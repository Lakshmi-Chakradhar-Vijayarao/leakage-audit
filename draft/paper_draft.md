## Abstract

Choosing a checkpoint, layer, threshold, or hyperparameter using the same data — or a statistic derived from it — later used to report performance inflates the reported metric. We show that this *selection-induced optimism* is real and code-verified in hidden-state hallucination-detection pipelines; that it is **small**, every code-verified AUROC-scale estimate landing in a band of roughly 0.000 to 0.034 AUROC once measured against matched controls; and that its size is governed not by *which* mechanism is responsible but by two continuous quantities — how many candidates were selected among (K), and the operating point the pipeline runs at. **Contrary to an earlier framing of this line of work, severity does not differ sharply by which mechanism is responsible.**

We identify five structurally distinct selection surfaces across two published pipelines and our own, three of them verified directly against pinned third-party source code: full-dataset fit-then-score feature leakage; cross-validation-based layer selection reported as held-out performance; training-loop checkpoint and scheduler coupling to a reused validation fold; test-set-driven best-layer selection; and test-set-tuned decision thresholds. Measured with matched controls — a placebo baseline, budget- and seed-matched clean conditions, and a bias-corrected, label-free calibration guardrail — their severities are: +0.0255 (SD 0.0186, 50 randomized splits) for CV-based layer selection; +0.0011 to +0.0036 for per-fold checkpoint selection on the primary synthetic sweep, rising to +0.0084 once a downstream feature-averaging step that attenuates it is removed, and to +0.034 on real features once the audited trainer's full optimizer schedule is ported; +0.0076 (BCa 95% CI [+0.0048,+0.0134], over the 17 of 24 published result files on which the estimator is non-degenerate) for test-set-driven best-layer selection; and exactly zero for test-set threshold selection, which is threshold-free-metric-invariant by construction while inflating F1 and accuracy by +0.021 to +0.034 (p<10^-31) at a test-set size of 140 — a figure that itself falls 6.2x by n_test=2000, since it is a winner's curse over a finite-sample criterion.

Holding the mechanism fixed, inflation grows with the number of candidates selected among (well fit by a+bln K, R^2=0.970, within a fixed operating point) and falls sharply with the operating point — by a factor of 48.6x, from +0.0093 at AUROC_0=0.70 to +0.0002 at 0.985. Fitting both axes at once on a purpose-built KxAUROC_0 factorial qualifies the first: ln K alone explains only R^2=0.038 across that grid, so the logarithmic law holds *within* a fixed operating point rather than in general, while the surface as a whole is well described by a *separable* multiplicative empirical fit, gappropto K^0.17e^-2.97 Phi^-1(AUROC_0) (R^2=0.976). While deriving these estimates we twice found our own measurement instrument committing the very pattern this paper audits for — first a full-dataset calibration transform evaluated across the split it was fit on, then a residual label-conditional step inside the fix for it — and replaced both with a fully label-free calibration that reaches chance exactly at zero separation.

**What we do not claim.** The 48.6x decline is a property of the synthetic harness that produced it, not a transportable severity law, and we test this rather than assume it: three measurements of the same contrast at achieved operating points within 0.003 AUROC of each other differ by 14x and 52x from what it predicts, because they differ in harness and in leakage mechanic. What survives is the weaker, methodologically load-bearing claim — the operating point is a strong severity modifier *within* a harness, so two severity numbers are not comparable until it is matched — which still explains why several estimates here come out near zero: they were measured on tasks already running near the ceiling. The multiplicative surface is an empirical fit, explicitly not a bound. We run no matched head-to-head across all five mechanisms at a common (K,AUROC_0), so we do not claim mechanism identity explains no residual variance. We give no field-wide prevalence estimate and no deployment-impact estimate, and we do not claim our largest single figure (+0.19 AUROC, Case Study 1) is verified — no artifact supporting it ships with this paper, and we exclude it from every comparison above. We also withdraw two claims made by earlier versions of this work. First, that the extreme-value form c sigma_valsqrt(2ln K) is falsified at R^2=-0.399: the sigma available in this harness is a training trajectory's dispersion rather than the sampling-noise SD that derivation requires, so the test cannot distinguish a wrong theory from a wrong measurement, and we now report it as untestable here. Second, that the two severity axes interact: that result came entirely from a K=3 column in which too few epochs elapse for the leaky and control arms to diverge at all, leaving them bitwise identical in 81–100% of folds, and it does not survive on a grid whose every cell is gated on a measured degeneracy check (p=0.027->0.193).

We release a code-verified taxonomy, this severity relationship, the label-free calibration guardrail, and an extension of the Kapoor & Narayanan / REFORMS leakage checklists. We also test whether the checklist can be automated: a regex scanner over 7 repositories yields zero true positives and misses both known bugs, for two causes we diagnose specifically rather than generalize from.

## 1. Introduction

Detecting hallucination by probing an LLM's internal hidden states is one of the most active subfields in LLM reliability research: a linear probe is cheap to train and needs no additional model calls, and reported AUROCs are often striking – 0.90 or higher, sometimes a perfect 1.000. This is also a setting with few samples and many dimensions (hundreds of samples, thousands of hidden dimensions), where high reported numbers should invite scrutiny of the evaluation protocol that produced them.

This paper began by accident. While building our own hidden-state hallucination detector (HaRP, targeting Qwen2.5-3B), we found and fixed a nested-cross-validation leakage bug in our own evaluation. That raised a question: is this an isolated mistake, or a structural hazard the broader literature shares? We find five *distinct* mechanisms by which evaluation labels can leak into a reported metric, and verify three of them (Mechanisms 3-5) directly against pinned commits of two externally published, code-available pipelines.

Measuring each of the five against matched controls — a placebo baseline, budget- and seed-matched clean conditions, and a bias-corrected calibration guardrail — then produced the result this paper is organized around, and it is not the one an earlier version of this work reported. That version argued severity "differs sharply by mechanism, not by pipeline." We no longer believe that. Every mechanism here with a code-verified AUROC-scale severity estimate produces inflation in a narrow band of roughly 0.000 to 0.034 AUROC. What severity *does* track, strongly and mechanism-independently, is two continuous quantities: the number of candidates selected among, and the operating point the pipeline runs at. Holding the mechanism fixed and varying only the task's calibrated difficulty moves the inflation by a factor of 48.6x — from +0.0093 at AUROC_0=0.70 to +0.0002 at 0.985. That relationship, previously buried in an appendix, is promoted here to a headline finding (§5) — but with a limit an earlier version asserted its way past. We previously said it moves severity "more than any difference we measure between mechanisms"; §5.4 now tests that against our own shipped data and it does not hold, two real-feature harnesses at achieved operating points within 0.003 AUROC of each other exceeding its prediction by 14x and 52x. The operating point is a strong severity modifier *within* a harness — enough to make matching it a precondition for comparing any two severity numbers, which is how this paper uses it — and not a law that transports across harnesses or mechanisms.

## 2. Related Work

**The definition we use.** *Leakage is a change in the expectation of a reported metric attributable to evaluation-target information entering the estimation process.* This is sharpened from the looser "evaluation-target information influencing a reported metric" an earlier version of this paper used, because two properties are load-bearing and this paper's own results violate the looser reading. First, the change is *signed*: it may be inflationary or deflationary, and in a given finite realization either sign can appear — the anisotropic sweep's capacity-384 CLEAN_MATCHED-minus-PLACEBO cell (§4.3) is negative, which a definition that presumes inflation cannot accommodate. Second, the change is *metric-relative*: leakage is a property of a (mechanism, metric) pair, not of a pipeline, so the same leak can move one reported metric while leaving another provably invariant — Mechanism 5 (§4.5) selects a decision threshold on the test labels, which inflates every threshold-dependent metric the same pipeline reports while having exactly zero effect on its AUROC, by construction rather than by measurement. Every severity number in this paper is therefore reported as a signed change in a named metric.

Leakage in this general sense is well documented in data mining (Kaufman et al. 2012) and in model-selection statistics: cross-validation-based hyperparameter or feature selection is optimistic unless nested inside the outer evaluation loop (Varma & Simon 2006). Case Studies 2 and 4 are direct instances of this general phenomenon in hidden-state probing; Case Studies 1 and 3 are more specific to the two-stage feature-extraction-then-classification structure common in this literature. To our knowledge, no prior work names this as a distinct pattern here.

**Existing leakage taxonomies and checklists.** The closest prior art is Kapoor & Narayanan (2023), who survey leakage across 17 scientific fields and 294 papers and propose an 8-type taxonomy, and REFORMS (Kapoor et al. 2024), a 32-item consensus checklist for ML-based science whose data-leakage module asks three questions. Mechanisms 4 and 5 are direct cases of Kapoor & Narayanan's **L1.3** — the test set is reused for a modeling decision and then for the reported metric; Mechanism 1 is an instance of L1.2, generalized from a preprocessing statistic to an entire fitted feature-extraction model; Mechanism 3 is a narrower-bandwidth variant of the same pattern; and all three REFORMS leakage questions are subsumed by the taxonomy here. Mechanism 2 (CV-based layer/hyperparameter selection optimism, i.e. Varma & Simon's problem) maps cleanly onto neither instrument, because both treat train/test separation and feature legitimacy as the leakage surface rather than iterative model selection against a validation signal — which is consistent with this paper's claim that hidden-state probing's two-stage structure surfaces at least one leakage pattern general cross-field taxonomies do not yet enumerate by name. The NeurIPS ML Reproducibility Checklist (Pineau et al. 2021) is reporting-practice-oriented rather than leakage-specific and would not, on its own, have caught any of the fourteen issues Appendix A documents in this paper's own instrument: the two kinds of checklist are complementary, not interchangeable, and a paper can satisfy the former while still committing the latter. Appendix D.1 gives the item-by-item comparison, including a mapping an earlier draft of this paper got wrong.

**Calibration and benchmark-construction critiques.** A large, separate literature asks whether LLM confidence is calibrated to correctness (surveys, hidden-state-based confidence estimation, conformal-prediction guarantees); that work treats the evaluation protocol as given, whereas our contribution sits upstream of it — an inflated AUROC corrupts any downstream calibration analysis built on it. Closer to our concern, PARALLAX (arXiv 2605.17028) audits 22 detection methods across 12 models and 6 corpora and finds four of six corpora leak the ground-truth answer directly into the input prompt, with most baselines falling to near chance once controlled for — independent, larger-scale evidence that the field's reported AUROCs are frequently protocol or construction artifacts, though its mechanism (benchmark construction) is distinct from all five documented here, each of which concerns a downstream selection step. Separately, surface-form correctness metrics (ROUGE-L, exact match) are known to diverge from LLM-judge or human factual-correctness judgments: a related but distinct label-quality concern this paper does not address. Fixing the leakage patterns we identify does not, by itself, address label-quality or benchmark-construction problems in the underlying dataset.

**Pretraining-data contamination.** A separate 2023-2026 thread asks whether a benchmark's answers appeared in an LLM's pretraining corpus (Shi et al. 2024; Golchin & Surdeanu 2024; Sainz et al. 2023) – a different failure mode from ours: contamination concerns what the model has already seen, not whether a downstream selection step used test-set signal after the model was already fixed. The two are complementary and, in principle, additive; we do not audit our four case studies' benchmarks for contamination.

To our knowledge, no prior paper both (a) names and distinguishes multiple, structurally different label-leakage mechanisms specific to hidden-state probing pipelines, and (b) quantifies their relative severity via controlled reconstruction rather than assuming all leakage is equally damaging.

## 3. Method: How These Case Studies Were Selected and Verified

Two case studies (§4.1-4.2) come from our own prior work, where we had full access to code, data, and commit history. The external case studies (§4.3-4.4) required three things: a real, runnable public code repository, verified directly via the repository host, not taken on faith from the paper's text; a reported AUROC in the range that warrants scrutiny (0.90 or higher); and an evaluation protocol whose leakage risk we could assess by reading the actual training/evaluation code, not just a methodology section. We did not limit the search to K-fold setups specifically, since one candidate paper's bug (§4.4) lives in a single train/val/test split with no K-fold structure at all – "avoid K-fold" is not itself a fix for this family of hazards.

**What "severity" means here, and why the numbers below are not all on one scale.** An earlier version used the single word "severity" for several quantities that are not comparable in absolute magnitude, inviting a reader to rank numbers that should not be ranked. We name them, and every reported severity carries a tag — e.g. "(in Delta_synth units)" — identifying which it is.

- Delta_synth — the **calibrated synthetic-harness gap**. Mean AUROC of LEAKY minus CLEAN_MATCHED over n=100 seeds, in a harness whose Bayes-optimal operating point is fixed by construction via the binormal identity (AUROC_0=0.80 unless stated), with both arms budget- and seed-matched and the operating point identical in every cell compared. Used in §4.3 and in every cell of §5's sweeps.

- Delta_real — the **real-feature gap**. The same LEAKY-minus-CLEAN_MATCHED difference, measured on real Mistral-7B/HaluEval features at whatever operating point those features and that harness actually land at (~0.94–0.95 in the checkpoint-selection harness, ~0.92 in the fidelity extension), which is *not* the synthetic harness's 0.80 and cannot be brought onto it (§4.3).

- Delta_sel — the **within-split selection-specific component**. For candidates 1,...,L each with a CV-vs-held-out optimism gap gap_l, and l^star the candidate the procedure's own argmax selects, Delta_sel = gap_l^star - frac1Lsum_l gap_l. This is a *contrast within one split*, not a difference between two experimental arms, and it is *relative to that split's own mean gap* rather than to an absolute clean baseline. Used for Case Study 2 (§4.2).

- Delta_wc — the **published-array winner's-curse estimate**, used only for Case Study 4 (§4.4): the selection-set estimate at the selected layer minus a genuinely held-out seed's estimate at that same layer, averaged over leave-one-seed-out rotations of already-published per-layer AUROC arrays. No model is retrained.

All four are differences of AUROC values, so they share *units* but not a *reference*. When this paper says severities "cluster" in a band of roughly 0.000 to 0.034 (§5, Conclusion), that describes the numerical range these quantities occupy, explicitly *not* a claim that they are interchangeable measurements of one underlying scale: Mechanism 5's F1 and accuracy gaps are on a different metric entirely and are never placed in that band, and because §5 shows the gap falls by 48.6x across the operating-point range, a Delta_real and a Delta_synth measured at different operating points are not comparable in magnitude either.

### 4.1 Case Study 1: full-dataset fit-then-score leakage (HaRP) — mechanism only, number not re-verifiable from this artifact

[See `draft/worked_examples.md`, Case Study 1, for full detail.]

**Evidentiary status: the numbers in this subsection are reported from our own prior work and are not reproducible from the materials submitted with this paper.** No script, log, or result file supporting them is included in this artifact. They are stated for completeness, because this bug is what started this paper, and because the mechanism is worth naming; they are excluded from the abstract's severity range and from every cross-mechanism comparison in this paper.

HaRP's Group-C representation feature `probe_conf` came from a logistic-regression probe fit on all 700 samples, which then scored those same 700 samples; only afterward did this feature enter a separate outer 5-fold cross-validation loop for the downstream failure estimator. This is nested-CV leakage in its cleanest form: the "held-out" fold's feature was manufactured by a model that had already seen every label, including that fold's own.

**Quantified effect.** A+B+C AUROC under 5-fold CV: 0.9620 (leaked) versus 0.7714 (fixed, out-of-fold) – a **+0.1906** inflation. `probe_conf` alone: in-sample AUROC 1.0000 versus proper out-of-fold AUROC 0.7573, independently cross-checked against a separately-obtained cross-validated AUROC of 0.7583 (agreement within 0.001).

**Fix:** refit both the probe and the class centroids per fold, using only that fold's training indices, scoring only the held-out indices.

### 4.2 Case Study 2: CV-based layer-selection optimism (GUARDIAN) — Delta_sel=+0.0255 AUROC after correction, original 18.8-point headline retracted

[See `draft/worked_examples.md`, Case Study 2, for full detail.]

GUARDIAN selects its detection layer – L11 of 32, on Mistral-7B – by argmax of a 5-fold cross-validated AUROC across all layers, then reports that same CV number as the model's performance.

**Quantified effect.** CV-selection AUROC at L11: 0.804. True held-out AUROC at L11 (trained on the first 400 samples, tested on a separate, never-selection-touched 300): **0.616** – an 18.8-point gap. Which layer counts as "optimal" also depends on the selection rule itself: argmax train-split CV picks L11; argmax all-data AUROC picks L19 instead. **Fix:** after CV-based selection, evaluate exactly once more on a disjoint held-out set that played no role in the selection.

**Full 32-layer decomposition, and a correction to it.** GUARDIAN's raw cache contains the complete (700, 32, 4096) Mistral-7B representations, so `code/48` can repeat the identical CV-vs-held-out comparison at all 32 layers under GUARDIAN's own sequential split. Under that split the mean optimism gap across all 32 layers is +0.192 (SD 0.053), indistinguishable from L11's own +0.188, with 18 of the other 31 layers showing a *larger* gap than L11 — which already says the 18.8-point figure is not specific to the selected layer (Appendix B.2).

**Correction: the sequential split confounds selection optimism with a population-difference artifact.** An adversarial review of this analysis found that GUARDIAN's `H[:400]`/`H[400:]` split is a fixed slice of whatever order the dataset happens to store samples in, not a random partition – and the two halves are separable by a hidden-state probe at AUROC 0.734–0.776, i.e. they are systematically different populations, not exchangeable draws. The quantity that isolates selection optimism specifically is the *selection-specific component* Delta_sel: gap-at-the-selected-layer minus the mean gap across all 32 layers, evaluated at whichever layer that split's own CV-argmax actually selects (a second review caught an earlier version hardcoding L11 for every check, including randomized reps that select a different layer).

\textbf{A mechanical property of Delta_sel a reader must not over-read.} It is positive in expectation even under a pure-noise null, since l^star=argmax_lcv_l while gap_l=cv_l-ho_l contains the same +cv_l term. That is not a defect — it is precisely the winner's curse the quantity is constructed to measure — but it means a measured Delta_sel's significance is *not* evidence of anything beyond argmax-over-a-noisy-statistic; what the magnitude adds is a scale for how large that expected effect is on this data (Appendix B.2).

Under the sequential split, Delta_sel at the selected layer is -0.004, indistinguishable from zero; reversing which half selects picks a different layer entirely (L17) and gives +0.074, a swing that is itself direct evidence the sequential-split number tracks split order rather than a stable property of the selection procedure. Recomputing under randomized stratified 400/300 splits, each rep evaluated at its own selected layer, gives Delta_sel=+0.0255 (SD 0.0186; one-sample t=9.71; Wilcoxon p=7.7x10^-12) — small but, for the first time, correctly measured and distinguishable from zero, about 7x smaller than the sequential split's apparent +0.188. The *general* CV-vs-held-out gap averaged across all 32 layers, by contrast, is +0.192 under the sequential split but only -0.0023 (SD 0.0541, t=-0.30, p=0.76) under the same randomized splits — indistinguishable from zero. Appendix B.2 gives the per-rep selected layers, which span 14 distinct layers from L5 to L23.

**What that p-value does and does not mean.** All 50 reps resample splits of the *same* 700 samples, so the SD is split-to-split variability conditional on one dataset, not sampling variability across independent draws, and the one-sample t-test's i.i.d. assumption is violated. Read its p as a within-dataset robustness statistic — "not an artifact of which particular split we took" — and *not* as evidence the effect generalizes beyond GUARDIAN's own 700-sample TruthfulQA sample; §4.4's Mechanism-4 estimate, drawn from 24 independent published result files, is the closest thing here to a replicated version of the same claim (Appendix B.3).

**A robustness check on the probe configuration.** The layer probe is `LogisticRegression` at d=4096, n=400, default C=1.0, on unscaled raw hidden states — reported as primary because it is what GUARDIAN itself ran. A review noted it could plausibly be so heavily regularized that both estimates are dominated by regularization rather than fit. It is not: the largest `n_iter_` across the 9,600 probe fits behind the primary result is 125, with zero `ConvergenceWarning`s, and adding `StandardScaler` while sweeping C over five decades leaves both conclusions intact — with the unscaled configuration, if anything, the mildly conservative one (Appendix B.4).

**The corrected conclusion reverses, not just narrows, the original claim**: the "large, real, sim19-point gap present at essentially every layer" was itself entirely an artifact of the sequential split's population-difference confound – under a proper random partition there is no general CV-vs-held-out optimism in this setup at all. What survives is narrower but genuine: a small (+0.0255), consistently positive, specifically selection-attributable penalty from CV-based argmax-over-layers selection, about 7x smaller than GUARDIAN's original headline number and now isolated from the artifact that previously made the two impossible to tell apart (`results/case_study_2_layer_decomposition.json`).

### 4.3 Case Study 3: per-fold checkpoint-selection leakage (MultiHaluDet) — Delta_synth=+0.0011 to +0.0036 AUROC on the primary synthetic sweep

[See `draft/case_study_multihaludet.md` for full code excerpts. Appendix A documents the fourteen-step correction history and the additional robustness checks behind this section's numbers; §4.3 states the result directly and nothing in Appendix A is required to verify it.]

MultiHaluDet (arXiv 2605.24919; repo `github.com/alvi-uiu/MultiHaluDet`, pinned at commit `c7597518`) reports up to 98.55% AUROC detecting hallucination from Mistral-7B/LLaMA-2-7B hidden states. Its training code (`run_pipeline.py::stage_2_3_train_oof`, `src/training/trainer.py::train_deep_model_fold`) selects each inner fold's kept checkpoint by argmax validation AUROC on that same fold, then stores that fold's features as "out-of-fold" for a downstream meta-learner. The checkpoint choice is a function of the validation fold's own labels; unlike Case Study 1, only the *checkpoint selection*, not gradient descent itself, sees the fold's labels.

#### 4.3.1 The primary synthetic estimate

Re-running MultiHaluDet's actual 7B-scale pipeline was outside this paper's compute budget, so we built a synthetic reconstruction mirroring its training loop exactly (5-fold structure, best-val-AUC checkpoint logic, OOF-feature downstream classifier), calibrated to a Bayes-optimal-linear ceiling of AUROC=0.80 via AUROC=Phi(sqrt(J/2)) (Simpson & Fitter 1973; J=1.417). Four conditions, n=100 seeds, four capacities (16/48/128/384 hidden units); all gaps below are in Delta_synth units. **LEAKY** chooses the checkpoint on the reused fold. **CLEAN_MATCHED** chooses it on a disjoint carve-out, then retrains from scratch on the full training budget for the resulting epoch count, using the same seed as LEAKY — isolating fold reuse from data budget. **PLACEBO** chooses it on permuted labels: zero *selection* signal, but real training signal, hence AUROC 0.73-0.74, not 0.5.

(A smaller asymmetry inside CLEAN_MATCHED, disclosed at a review's request: its epoch count is selected by a model trained on only 85% of the fold's training data, while the retrain that produces the reported model uses 100%. The two arms are matched on the retrain's data budget, which is what this control was built for, but not on the data budget of the run the epoch count was chosen from. Appendix A.2 states why its direction is not determined a priori and why we disclose rather than correct it.)

#### 4.3.2 Alternative explanations, tested directly

**Is it "having adaptive checkpoint selection at all," rather than fold reuse?** A fifth condition with the full training budget and the identical *ongoing* adaptive selection LEAKY uses, but selecting against a disjoint carve-out (`code/22`), does not approach LEAKY: at capacity 128 LEAKY beats it by +0.0198 (p<0.0001), *larger* than the original LEAKY-CLEAN_MATCHED gap rather than smaller, and the pattern holds at 384 (+0.0143, p<0.0001). The alternative is directionally unsupported — but this control retains only 37.3% and 25.7% of honest selection's placebo-relative benefit at those two capacities, and selects against a carve-out 1.67x smaller than LEAKY's fold, so we do not treat it as having ruled the alternative out (Appendix A.3).

**Is the measured severity small only because the pipeline averages it away?** The harness — like MultiHaluDet's own `run_pipeline.py:108` that it mirrors — averages five fold-models' test-set activations before the meta-learner scores them, so a fold-specific leaked signal could be largely cancelled before the reported metric ever sees it. **That alternative is supported, and we report it as such.** Reading the *same* trained fold-models without the averaging (`code/55`) raises the gap at every capacity by roughly 2.3–5.8x, to +0.0073/+0.0066/+0.0084/+0.0084 at capacities 16/48/128/384, and makes it significant at all four rather than two. We keep both readings: the averaged number is the correct estimate of inflation in what the audited pipeline actually reports, so the table above stands as this paper's primary estimate, while the un-averaged number is the correct estimate of the mechanism's severity at its source, about 3x larger. The honest summary is therefore narrower than "this mechanism is small": it is small *as reported*, partly because a downstream ensembling step attenuates it, and a pipeline with the same leak but without that step should be expected to show more (Appendix A.4).

**Does the estimate depend on the generative process?** The primary sweep draws each class from an isotropic Gaussian, so a toy-model artifact is a live alternative, and two further experiments address it. A second generative process built on the real, anisotropic within-class covariance of the Mistral-7B/HaluEval features, rescaled to the same AUROC=0.80 target (`code/27`), gives +0.0082 (p=0.007) at capacity 128 and +0.0099 (p=0.009) at 384 while weakening at the two lower capacities; pooling all eight synthetic LEAKY-vs-CLEAN_MATCHED tests into one Holm-Bonferroni family leaves two significant, both capacity-128 cells. A third separates covariance shape from dimensionality, holding d=64, the eigenbasis, the total within-class variance, the mean-difference direction and the calibrated Mahalanobis J all fixed and varying *only* the eigenvalue profile (`code/56`): severity at capacity 128 then rises monotonically with anisotropy, from +0.0015 (beta=0, an exact isotropic control, not significant) through +0.0038 and +0.0044 to +0.0104 (beta=2), with `real_top64` at +0.0048. The isotropic assumption alone is therefore not responsible for the severity estimate. **Two limits on that reading, which we state rather than absorb:** the evidence is weaker than "replicates and is larger" would suggest, because the capacity-by-capacity significance pattern reshuffles rather than replicating cleanly and because the pooled verdict moved in our own favour when our own instrument was corrected — which deserves the most scrutiny, not the least; and anisotropy is perfectly rank-confounded with the achieved operating point in the third experiment (Spearman -1.00 at capacity 128, no monotone pattern at all at 384), so it cannot distinguish "anisotropy raises severity" from "anisotropy lowers the operating point, and a lower operating point raises severity." We do not claim to have distinguished them. Appendices A.5 and A.6 give both in full.

#### 4.3.3 Real-feature validation, and a leak in our own instrument

Feeding real Mistral-7B/HaluEval features (n=400, MultiHaluDet's own unmodified feature-extraction code, a 4-bit AWQ checkpoint pinned to a fixed revision) through the same 5-fold out-of-fold-plus-meta-learner architecture gives LEAKY beating CLEAN_MATCHED by only +0.0002 AUROC (p=0.56, n=100 seeds) at capacity 128 and +0.0001 (p=0.96) at capacity 384. **That comparison is not usable as reported: every condition operates at AUROC ~0.985 on these raw real features** – a task with no room to fail leaves no room for a leakage bug to show inflation either.

Calibrating them down to the synthetic sweep's operating point took four rounds of correction, **two of which found this paper's own measurement instrument committing the very pattern it audits for**: a rescaling transform that centered class means on the *full* labeled sample before any split existed — mechanically forcing the two halves' leftover class-mean directions into an algebraic identity, verified at alpha=0 as cos(Deltamu_train,Deltamu_test)=-0.9999 with a train-fit, test-scored AUROC of 0.06 — and then, inside the obvious fix for it, a residual label-conditional step that still read each test point's own label to decide which class offset to subtract, leaving a train-fit-> test-eval AUROC of 0.584+/-0.059 at alpha=0, where chance is the correct answer. Both are exactly the pattern §6's checklist names. The replacement is a fully label-free calibration — class-mean axis, midpoint and pooled within-class SD estimated from train indices only, then one identical noising map applied to every row — which reaches chance exactly at zero separation (0.487+/-0.092). Appendix A.7 derives it, together with a simpler label-free candidate that provably cannot work and why that negative result is load-bearing; Appendix A (issues 5, 6, 8, 9, 13) records the correction history.

**The re-derived result.** The alpha search converges to alpha=0.3359, achieving CV AUROC 0.7995 against the 0.80 target — for the first time in this test's history the calibration reaches its intended operating point rather than saturating above it. Under the downstream OOF-plus-meta-learner pipeline the operating point still lands at ~0.94-0.95 (LEAKY 0.9403 at capacity 128, 0.9460 at 384), because that stack recovers discriminative structure in directions other than the single axis the calibration operates on, so a direct severity-magnitude comparison against the synthetic sweep's 0.80 harness remains invalid by this paper's own stated principle and we do not make one. At its own achieved operating point the test gives (in Delta_real units) LEAKY beating CLEAN_MATCHED by +0.0093 (BCa 95% CI [+0.0060,+0.0134], capacity 128) and +0.0077 (CI [+0.0050,+0.0109], capacity 384), strongly significant under both tests we report (Wilcoxon p=7.3x10^-7 and 1.4x10^-5, paired permutation p<0.0001), which now agree where previously they did not. CLEAN_MATCHED beats PLACEBO decisively at both capacities (+0.0151, p=9.4x10^-7; +0.0091, p=1.0x10^-5).

These paired differences contain many tied absolute values (33 of 100 at capacity 128, 40 of 100 at 384) because AUROC on an 80-sample test split is discrete, and the Wilcoxon statistic degrades under ties; we therefore report the paired sign-flip permutation test alongside it throughout (Appendix A.7). A separate FP16-vs-AWQ control finds identical AUROC (0.9600 both) at a point estimate, with a resampled 95% CI of [-0.061,+0.080] — consistent with no quantization confound rather than a demonstration ruling one out at this resolution (Appendix A.10). This paper's primary severity estimate for Mechanism 3 still rests on the two synthetic sweeps, which draw fresh samples from a fully-specified generative process each seed rather than rescaling a small fixed real sample, and so share neither the analytic-bias nor the split-leakage problem found here.

#### 4.3.4 A fidelity extension: the audited trainer does more than pick a checkpoint

Everything above ports only MultiHaluDet's final "keep the best-val-AUROC checkpoint" decision. Its real trainer also steps a `ReduceLROnPlateau` scheduler on that same fold's AUROC every epoch and ties early stopping to the same signal — a continuous reactive coupling, not a single argmax. Porting that mechanic in, with a control that keeps the same scheduler and early-stopping mechanic but feeds it a genuinely disjoint carve-out (`code/49`), gives LEAKY beating that control by Delta_real=+0.0338 (BCa 95% CI [+0.0279,+0.0405], Wilcoxon p=6.2x10^-15, permutation p<0.0001, n=100 seeds, capacity 128) — about 3.6x the checkpoint-selection-only harness's +0.0093 on the same features under the same calibration, at an operating point that now matches it closely (LEAKY mean 0.9424 versus 0.9403).

**Two scope limits on this number.** *(i)* The two arms differ in training depth as well as in validation signal — LEAKY's kept checkpoint sits at a mean epoch of 29.14 against the control's 19.75, +47.6% (Wilcoxon p=7.2x10^-13), with the placebo arm stopping earliest at 7.95. We report rather than equalize this, because that depth is chosen by the same signal and choosing when to stop *is* the leaked decision this extension models; but the arms are not matched on it. *(ii)* "Fidelity" here means fidelity of the validation-signal coupling and the optimizer schedule, not of the loss or the model: a review found this extension silently differed from the audited trainer on most of its optimizer and data-pipeline settings, which are now ported and verified against the pinned commit, while the weight EMA, the composite objective, the class rebalancing and augmentation, and the model class itself remain unported. So this estimates how much continuous scheduler and early-stopping coupling adds over a final-checkpoint argmax *within this paper's own harness*, not MultiHaluDet's own reported number's inflation. Appendix A.8 gives the exhaustive ported/not-ported list and the 2x2 ablation separating this extension's two most recent corrections.

#### 4.3.5 A pre-registered decision rule, and the bottom line

A rule pre-registered before the sweep, classifying each capacity as `GENUINE_LEAK_CONFIRMED`, `CONFOUND_CONFIRMED_NO_REAL_LEAK` or `MIXED`, turns out to be reachable in every direction but not *stable*: the same mechanism produces all three verdicts depending on generative process, calibration and capacity, and the verdict moved in the *favourable* direction for our own hypothesis when the calibration was corrected. That is precisely when a single pre-registered label deserves least weight, and it is why this paper's severity claims rest on effect sizes with intervals (Appendix A.9).

**Bottom line.** The mechanism – checkpoint choice as a function of the reused fold's own labels – is unambiguous and code-verified. Its quantified severity, once budget- and seed-matched, is small (+0.0011 to +0.0036 AUROC on the synthetic sweep), and remains small under a second generative process with real anisotropic covariance and under a leak-free real-feature harness (+0.0077 to +0.0093 AUROC at that harness's own ~0.94-0.95 operating point, which is not comparable in magnitude to the synthetic sweep's 0.80). Of the two alternative explanations tested, "any adaptive selection helps" is not supported, though the control testing it is underpowered; and cross-model feature averaging *is* attenuating the measured number, by 2.3–5.8x. None of this settles MultiHaluDet's own 98.55% AUROC's exact inflation: their real architecture (6 transformer layers, multi-scale, heavy augmentation) is considerably more expressive than this reconstruction.

### 4.4 Case Study 4: test-set-driven best-layer selection (quantized-LLM paper) — Delta_wc=+0.0076 AUROC over the 17 of 24 cells whose estimator is non-degenerate

[See `draft/case_study_quantized_llm_paper.md` for full detail, including literal code excerpts.]

"Hallucination Is Linearly Decodable from Mid-Layer Hidden States in Quantized LLMs" (Aiersilan 2026; arXiv 2606.02628; repo `github.com/Ezharjan/HallucinationPatternDetection`, pinned at commit `ea0b9678`) reports AUROC up to 1.000. Its per-layer probe training (`src/detection/probes.py::train_probe`) uses a clean single 70/10/20 train/val/test split, genuinely free of Case Studies 1-3's exact patterns. But `src/detection/saplma.py::saplma_probe_per_layer` selects a `best_layer` by the argmax, over every probed layer, of each layer's **test-set** AUROC, then reports that same test-set number as the headline result with no correction for having tried that many hypotheses against the one test set — the classical "winner's curse" of multiple-hypothesis selection using the exact metric later reported. The number of probed layers is model-dependent and not constant across the 24 cells, as an earlier version of this section incorrectly implied: 33 for the eight `llama3.1-8b` and eight `mistral-7b` cells, 29 for the eight `qwen2.5-7b` cells, with `code/45` asserting these counts against the shipped files so the figure cannot drift.

Re-quantifying its severity does not require re-running their quantized checkpoint from scratch, as we first believed: their repo ships per-layer, per-seed AUROC arrays for all 24 model/dataset/probe-type combinations (`code/external/HallucinationPatternDetection/results/probes/*.json`; 3 seeds each). For each combination (`code/45`), each seed is held out in turn, the best layer is selected using *only* the other two, the naive baseline is the selection-set mean at that layer, and the honest estimate is the held-out seed's AUROC at the same layer; their difference, averaged over the three rotations, is the winner's-curse estimate.

**The estimator is algebraically degenerate on 7 of the 24 cells, and we report around that rather than through it.** Whenever all three rotations select the same layer the rotation estimate is pinned to exactly zero *for any data*: the per-rotation values are 1.5x the held-out seed's deviation from the seed mean, pure noise that sums to zero by construction. In those cells the estimator is not measuring a small effect; it is measuring nothing. `code/45` therefore detects and declares degeneracy per cell, reports the headline over the 17 non-degenerate cells only with the 7 degenerate ones disclosed rather than averaged in, and characterizes the estimator *including* its degeneracy against a within-layer permutation null — the regime of *maximal* winner's curse, which answers the question the degenerate cells cannot — with a structurally different bootstrap max-bias estimator as a sign-and-magnitude cross-check. This matters because all seven degenerate cells fall in the non-saturated subgroup, the subgroup §5 leans on (Appendix B.5).

**The corrected numbers.** Over the 17 non-degenerate cells, Delta_wc=+0.0076 AUROC (BCa 95% CI [+0.0048,+0.0134], Wilcoxon p=1.5x10^-5; max +0.0347). The 7 degenerate cells are excluded and are exactly 0.0000 by construction; their permutation null averages +0.0179, i.e. *above* the non-degenerate cells' observed mean — so those exact zeros reflect a stable argmax defeating the estimator, not an absent selection effect. The contaminated all-24-cell mean is +0.0054 (CI [+0.0031,+0.0101], p=0.00029), which we retain only for continuity with the previous revision and label as contaminated wherever it appears. The bootstrap max-bias cross-check agrees in sign everywhere and averages +0.0027 over the non-degenerate cells, smaller than the rotation estimate as expected at three seeds.

**Excluding the degenerate cells is itself a selection, and we state which way it cuts.** A cell is degenerate exactly when its argmax is stable across all three rotations, and a stable argmax is evidence the layer ranking there is driven by real structure rather than noise — the regime in which a winner's curse is *genuinely smaller*. The 17 surviving cells are therefore plausibly enriched for a larger true effect, and +0.0076 should be read as **the severity among cells where this estimator has resolving power, not as an unbiased average over all 24**. That unbiased quantity is not recoverable from three seeds by any estimator of this shape; the honest bracket is that the true all-cell mean lies between the contaminated +0.0054 (which assumes the degenerate cells are exactly zero, and they are not) and +0.0076 (which assumes they resemble the cells the estimator can see, and they probably do not). We report both and do not split the difference. **Power:** the 24 per-cell estimates have SD 0.0079, so the pooled design's minimum detectable effect at alpha=0.05, 80% power is +0.0045; the non-degenerate non-saturated subgroup is n=5 and plainly underpowered, so we report it descriptively and do not lean on its p=0.0625 (Appendix B.5).

**Ceiling composition of the 24 cells.** These cells are not comparable to each other, and reporting only their pooled mean hides that. In 12 of 24 the *selected* layer already sits at AUROC >=0.975, leaving almost no headroom for a selection effect; the remaining 12 are genuinely non-saturated. Reported separately, with the degeneracy split shown rather than buried: ceiling-saturated +0.0042 (CI [+0.0029,+0.0057], 12 cells, *none* degenerate); non-saturated +0.0065 (CI [+0.0022,+0.0154], p=0.0625) across all 12 of its cells, but +0.0157 (CI [+0.0074,+0.0276], p=0.0625) across the 5 the estimator can actually see. **The honest reading of that contrast is therefore the second pair**, a 3.7x difference rather than what the contaminated numbers suggested. Severity is small on every slicing and larger where there is room for it to show, consistent with §5's operating-point relationship — whose transport limits §5.4 reports (Appendix B.6).

### 4.5 Mechanism 5: test-set-driven operating-point selection (MultiHaluDet) — found while auditing Case Study 3's own repository

A fresh, independent review found a fifth, structurally distinct leakage mechanism in the exact file this paper already audits for Mechanism 3. `MultiHaluDet/run_pipeline.py::stage_4_ensemble` calls `find_best_thresholds(probs, y_test)` (`src/utils/metrics.py`), which sweeps 81 candidate F1 thresholds and the ROC-optimal (Youden) threshold *directly against the test labels*, then reports every threshold-dependent metric — F1, accuracy, Matthews correlation, Cohen's kappa, balanced accuracy, and the ECE computed from those predictions — at that test-label-selected threshold. AUROC is threshold-free and unaffected, but every other headline number MultiHaluDet reports is test-set-optimized operating-point selection: the choice of *where to draw the decision boundary* uses the same labels the boundary is then scored against. This is structurally distinct from Mechanisms 1-4, which all concern *which feature, checkpoint, or layer* to use; this one concerns *which threshold to report at*, given a fixed, already-chosen model.

**It is also not specific to this repository, which we learned only after writing this section.** An independent blind re-read of both audited repositories (§6) found the same pattern in Case Study 4's: `HallucinationPatternDetection/src/analysis/metrics.py:57-65` picks a Youden threshold by `np.argmax(tpr-fpr)` on the same labels it then scores `accuracy_at_best_thr` and `f1_at_best_thr` against, and `scripts/07_aggregate_results.py:85-86` reports both into the headline table, verified against the pinned commit. Two independently published pipelines committing the identical operating-point-selection pattern is what makes Mechanism 5 worth naming separately; a single instance would not have been.

We quantified this severity using the same isotropic-Gaussian calibration this paper's other severity estimates rely on, with a simple logistic-regression classifier in place of the full architecture — threshold-selection leakage does not depend on which classifier produced the probabilities (`code/46`). **Correction:** an earlier version of this generator itself committed the exact calibration-inversion error class Appendix A (issue 11) documents elsewhere in this paper's own tooling, silently quadrupling the realized Fisher J relative to the labeled operating point; it was caught by a subsequent independent review and fixed, verified via a dedicated, non-tautological calibration guardrail (`code/sanity_checks.py`). Comparing a threshold selected on the test labels (`find_best_thresholds`, ported verbatim from MultiHaluDet) against one selected on an independent validation split never touching the test labels, across five operating points spanning this paper's other severity estimates: F1 gaps range from +0.0212 (0.985, MultiHaluDet's own reported regime) to +0.0338 (target 0.80), with no clean monotonic trend (0.70->0.0228, 0.80->0.0338, 0.90->0.0311, 0.95->0.0233, 0.985->0.0212), all significant at p<10^-31 over 200 seeds with BCa 95% CIs excluding zero throughout; accuracy gaps are comparable (+0.0215 to +0.0457).

**These gaps are a function of sample size, and the sample sizes were fixed and unstated.** All five cells above run at N=700, hence n_test=140. A threshold chosen by argmax over an 81-point grid is a winner's curse over a *noisy* criterion whose noise scales with 1/sqrt(n), so this magnitude is not a property of the mechanism alone — and "MultiHaluDet's own reported regime" above matches their operating point and *not* their sample size, which their released configuration does not pin. Holding the operating point at 0.985 and scaling N gives an F1 gap of +0.0212 at n_test=140, +0.0111 at 350, +0.0067 at 700 and +0.0034 at 2000 — a **6.2x decline**, close to the 3.8x a pure 1/sqrt(n) account would give. Every cell remains overwhelmingly significant, so the mechanism does not vanish, but \textbf{the +0.021 to +0.034 range quoted here is an estimate at n_test=140, and a pipeline reporting at ten times that size should expect roughly a third of it} (Appendix B.7).

**A necessary caveat on comparing this to Mechanism 3: these are not the same metric.** AUROC is threshold-free by construction, so test-set threshold selection has *exactly zero* effect on it — Mechanism 5's severity on the one metric it shares with Mechanism 3 is not small but precisely nil. The F1 gap is also structurally guaranteed non-negative: LEAKY's threshold is an argmax over the very grid the test F1 is then scored on, so it cannot do worse than any other threshold on that grid, HONEST's included. An earlier draft compared these F1/accuracy gaps directly against Mechanism 3's AUROC gaps and described Mechanism 5 as "an order of magnitude more severe"; that conflated two different metrics measuring two different things and has been removed. The honest statement is narrower: Mechanism 5 leaves the pipeline's reported AUROC entirely unaffected but materially inflates every threshold-dependent metric (F1, accuracy, MCC, Cohen's kappa, and any ECE computed from those threshold-derived predictions) that MultiHaluDet also reports at that same selected threshold — a distinct, real leak in its own right, not a directly rankable "more or less severe than Mechanism 3."

## 5. What Actually Governs Severity: Candidate Count and Operating Point

The five mechanisms above are structurally distinct, and identifying them is this paper's most durable contribution. But once each is measured against matched controls, the striking result is how *little* their severities differ from one another — and how much they differ across conditions that have nothing to do with which mechanism is responsible. This section states that finding, and then bounds it.

### 5.1 The five mechanisms are points in a shared space, not disjoint kinds

Three axes distinguish them: **(a) the provenance of the statistic selected on** — full-dataset labels, an inner cross-validation fold, or the test set; **(b) the object selected** — a feature, a layer, a training checkpoint, or a decision threshold; and **(c) whether the selection's output is itself reused downstream as a feature**, as opposed to only fixing a modelling choice.

| Mechanism | (a) Statistic's provenance | (b) Object selected | (c) Reused as a feature? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (HaRP) | full-dataset labels | a feature (probe score) | yes |
| 2 (GUARDIAN) | CV over the selection pool | a layer | no |
| 3 (MultiHaluDet) | inner CV validation fold | a training checkpoint | yes |
| 4 (quantized-LLM) | the test set | a layer | no |
| 5 (MultiHaluDet) | the test set | a decision threshold | no |

\caption{The five mechanisms located on three axes. The overlaps are the point: these are documented instances, not an orthogonal partition.} \end{table}

**These are documented instances, not an orthogonal partition, and we do not claim otherwise.** Mechanisms 2 and 4 share axes (b) and (c) and differ *only* on (a); 4 and 5 share (a) and (c) and differ only on (b); 1 and 3 share (c), the property that makes both feed a downstream estimator. Nor are they mutually exclusive in practice: Mechanisms 3 and 5 co-occur inside a single file of a single audited repository (`MultiHaluDet/run_pipeline.py`), which is how we found Mechanism 5 at all. There is no partition criterion under which these are five disjoint kinds. That is not a weakness for the taxonomy's intended use — the axes are what tell an auditor *where to look* — and it is arguably predicted by this section's finding: if severity is governed by continuous quantities rather than by discrete mechanism identity, one should expect the mechanisms to be points along shared axes and neighbouring points to behave similarly.

### 5.2 The measured severities cluster

Every mechanism in this paper with a code-verified AUROC-scale estimate lands in a narrow band. Under randomized splits, Mechanism 2's selection-specific component is Delta_sel=+0.0255 (SD 0.0186). Mechanism 3's checkpoint-selection severity is Delta_synth=+0.0011 to +0.0036 on the isotropic synthetic sweep and +0.0000 to +0.0099 on the anisotropic sweep, and Delta_real=+0.0077 to +0.0093 on the real-feature harness; extending it to model the audited trainer's full LR-scheduler-plus-early-stopping coupling raises the latter (§4.3). Mechanism 4's is Delta_wc=+0.0076 across the 17 of 24 published cells whose estimator is non-degenerate (+0.0157 on the non-saturated, non-degenerate five; §4.4). Mechanism 5's effect on AUROC is exactly zero by construction, while its effect on the threshold-dependent metrics the same pipeline reports is +0.021 to +0.034 in F1 at n_test=140 (and about a third of that at n_test=2000; §4.5). That is a spread of roughly 0.000 to 0.034 AUROC across four structurally unrelated leakage surfaces in two independent published pipelines — all small, and none separated from the others by anything like the margin an "it depends which leak you have" framing would need.

### 5.3 Two continuous quantities move severity far more than mechanism identity does

`code/47` varies, one at a time and with the leakage mechanism held fixed, the number of candidates selected among (K), the selection-set size (n_val), and the operating point (the calibrated Bayes-optimal AUROC):

- **Candidate count.** Sweeping Kin1,...,225 moves the gap monotonically from +0.0000 to +0.0052, well described by gap=a+bln K (R^2=0.970). This is an empirical logarithmic regularity and *not* a confirmation of the sigma_valsqrt(2ln K) extreme-value form — but neither is it a refutation of one, and an earlier version of this paper claimed the latter: the only sigma this harness produces is a *single training trajectory's* per-epoch dispersion, not the sampling-noise SD of K exchangeable estimates the derivation requires, so the fit tests the wrong quantity and cannot separate "the theory is wrong" from "this is the wrong sigma". We report the extreme-value form as **untestable in this harness as instrumented**, not as falsified (Appendix A.11). The sweep's *achieved* operating point also drifts, from 0.6269 at K=1 to 0.7574 at K=225, so the full-range fit is partly confounded with the operating-point relationship below — but the law survives restriction to K>=10, where that drift spans only 0.7565–0.7616 (b=0.00107, R^2=0.955, versus b=0.00104, R^2=0.970 over the full range), which is why we disclose the confound rather than qualify the result.

- **Operating point.** This is the largest effect measured *inside this harness*. Holding the mechanism, K, and n_val fixed and varying only the calibrated difficulty of the task, the gap falls from +0.0093 at AUROC_0=0.70 to +0.0036/+0.0011/+0.0007 at 0.80/0.90/0.95 and to +0.0002 at 0.985 — a **48.6x decline**, one and a half orders of magnitude, driven by nothing but where on the difficulty curve the pipeline operates. **This decline does not transport outside the harness that produced it, and §5.4 is the test of that** — a test an earlier version of this paper omitted while nonetheless promoting the 48.6x figure as though it were general.

- **Selection-set size — and a correction to how this sweep was labelled.** An earlier version reported an "n_val sweep" that was nothing of the kind: it varied N_SAMPLES, which moves the training set, the test set *and* the per-fold validation set together. It is relabelled a *sample-size* sweep, with that confound stated. The isolated version holds N_SAMPLES=700 fixed and varies only the inner CV fold count, which is what sets n_val (`code/47`, Sweep D). It gives a *monotone non-increasing* relationship, in the direction *opposite* to the winner's-curse prediction: +0.0087 at n_val=280, +0.0036 at 186 and again at 112 (tied to within 3x10^-5), and +0.0005 (not significant, p=0.22) at 56. It is not that the selection signal fails to get noisier — the measured sigma_val moves as the theory expects; the gap simply does not follow it. What it follows is the operating point, which rises monotonically across exactly these cells because a smaller n_val means a larger per-fold training set. That residual coupling is inherent to cross-validation and we disclose it rather than claim complete isolation, but it makes this sweep further evidence for the operating-point account and against the 1/sqrtn_val one. We no longer describe any n_val relationship as "directionally consistent with the winner's-curse account" (per-cell values in Appendix A.11).

### 5.4 Does the operating-point relationship transport? No — by a factor of 14 to 52

A later independent review observed that we promote the 48.6x decline to the abstract, the contributions list and the Conclusion, at one point as moving severity "more than any difference we measure between mechanisms," while never performing the one test our own JSONs make available. Three measurements of the *same* LEAKY-minus-CLEAN_MATCHED contrast sit at achieved operating points within 0.0030 AUROC of one another, and their severities differ by more than an order of magnitude (`code/58`, `results/operating_point_transport_check.json`):

| Harness | Leakage mechanic | Achieved AUROC | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Synthetic Sweep C (`code/47`) | checkpoint argmax only | 0.9433 | +0.00065 |
| Real features (`code/43`, cap. 128) | checkpoint argmax only | 0.9403 | +0.0093 |
| Fidelity extension (`code/49`, cap. 128) | argmax + LR sched. + ES | 0.9424 | +0.0338 |

\caption{The operating-point relationship does not transport. Three harnesses matched to within 0.0030 AUROC differ in severity by more than an order of magnitude.} \end{table}

Taking Sweep C's cell as "what the relationship predicts at ~0.94," the real-feature checkpoint-only harness comes in at 14.3x that prediction and the fidelity extension at 51.8x. Against a probit-space log-linear interpolation of Sweep C evaluated at each harness's exact achieved operating point — so the comparison cannot be dismissed as an artifact of rounding to the nearest grid cell — the ratios are 15.1x and 56.9x.

**What we therefore claim, and what we withdraw.** We withdraw the general reading. The 48.6x decline is a property of the synthetic harness that produced it — its isotropic generative process, its single fixed leakage mechanic — and is *not* a severity law that transports across harnesses or across mechanisms at a matched operating point. The sentence "that single relationship moves severity more than any difference we measure between mechanisms" is withdrawn: the table above is precisely a difference between mechanisms and harnesses, at a matched operating point, larger than the operating-point relationship predicts by 14–52x. What survives, and what we do claim, is weaker and more useful: **the operating point is a strong severity modifier *within* a harness, and two severity numbers measured at different operating points are not comparable until it is matched.** That is enough to support this paper's actual methodological use of the relationship — explaining why ceiling-saturated cells show near-zero severity, and forbidding the direct synthetic-versus-real magnitude comparison we already decline to make (§4.3) — and not enough to predict any pipeline's severity from its operating point alone. Note also that the three rows differ in mechanic as well as harness, so this comparison does not separate the two; that is itself the point, since the withdrawn claim was that the operating point dominates mechanism differences.

### 5.5 Do the two axes combine into one function? An empirical joint fit

An independent review observed that this paper reports two strong one-at-a-time regularities and never tries to combine them, and that a joint function phi(K, n_sel, AUROC_0) upper-bounding the expected inflation would unify the five mechanisms and turn §6's checklist into a calculator. It was right that we had not attempted it. We attempt it here, and we are explicit that what follows is an **empirical fit, not a bound and not a theorem** (Limitations (2a)). The existing sweeps cannot answer it: Sweep A varies K at a fixed AUROC_0=0.80 and Sweep C varies AUROC_0 at a fixed K=45, so together they form a *cross* through the plane rather than a grid, and no cross can exhibit an interaction. `code/57` runs the missing cells: a full Kin15,45,135,405xAUROC_0in0.70,0.80,0.90,0.95,0.985 factorial in the same harness, n=100 seeds per cell, n_val held fixed at 112 throughout (Sweep D shows it is not an independent third axis here). The K=45 column reproduces Sweep C exactly.

**A degenerate corner of the first version of this grid, and why K=3 is no longer in it.** The grid originally ran Kin3,15,45,135, and on it the interaction term below was significant (F(1,16)=5.92, p=0.027), which an earlier version of this paper promoted to the abstract as "the two axes interact multiplicatively rather than additively." That result came *entirely* from the K=3 column, which is degenerate: with three candidates the two selection rules almost never diverge, the arms come out *bitwise identical* in 81–100% of folds, and the "gap" there is 0.00000 by construction rather than by measurement. Refitting the old grid without that column moves the interaction test from p=0.027 to p=0.378, while dropping any *other* K column leaves it significant — the interaction was a property of the degenerate corner, not of the surface. So K=3 is replaced by K=405; every cell now carries a **degeneracy record** with the fit *gated* on it; the old column ships, re-run under the same instrumentation, as evidence for its own removal; and every conclusion below is refit five ways, with each K column deleted in turn, with the range reported rather than a single number. Appendix C.1 gives the per-column degeneracy fractions — which decay with K rather than switching off, so the gate is not a clean bill of health — the drop-one-column sensitivity, and a disclosure about one configuration that legitimately has two values in this paper.

| AUROC_0 | K=15 | K=45 | K=135 | K=405 | span across K |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.70 | +0.00604 | +0.00932 | +0.00922 | +0.01100 | 1.8x |
| 0.80 | +0.00117 | +0.00365 | +0.00366 | +0.00496 | 4.2x |
| 0.90 | +0.00015 | +0.00109 | +0.00096 | +0.00105 | 7.1x |
| 0.95 | +0.00015 | +0.00065 | +0.00046 | +0.00057 | 4.3x |
| 0.985 | +0.00012 | +0.00019 | +0.00016 | +0.00016 | 1.6x |

\caption{The KxAUROC_0 severity surface (n=100 seeds per cell, n_val=112). Candidate count matters where there is headroom and essentially not at all at AUROC_0=0.985 — which is why the K-law is a within-operating-point regularity, not a general one.} \end{table}

Fitting the 20 cell means with z_0=Phi^-1(AUROC_0) — the coordinate in which this harness's own binormal calibration is linear, so a linear term in it is the parsimonious choice rather than a fitted one — gives, reporting leave-one-cell-out predictive R^2 alongside in-sample R^2 because 20 cells against 4 parameters invites overfitting: gap=a+bln K alone, R^2=0.038 (LOO -0.192); a+cz_0 alone, R^2=0.650 (LOO 0.555); the additive a+bln K+cz_0, R^2=0.688 (LOO 0.547); with an interaction term added, R^2=0.720 (LOO 0.512) — and **the interaction is not significant** (F(1,16)=1.85, p=0.193), with the leave-one-cell-out R^2 *falling* when it is added, which is what an overfit term looks like; and a multiplicative form gap=exp(a+bln K+cz_0), i.e. gappropto K^0.17e^-2.97 z_0, R^2=0.976 (LOO 0.948).

**What this says.** A single joint model does describe this surface well, and it is the multiplicative one: severity factors, to a good approximation, into a candidate-count term and an operating-point term multiplied together, gap~ A* K^be^cz_0. That form is *separable* — it contains no interaction term — so its excellence relative to the additive model is a statement about the *scale* on which the two axes combine, not evidence that they interact, and **we therefore withdraw the previous version's claim that "the two axes interact multiplicatively rather than additively."** The conclusion survives the test that killed its predecessor: deleting each K column in turn leaves the interaction non-significant every time and the multiplicative fit strong throughout, with a stable operating-point coefficient and an unstable candidate-count exponent (Appendix C.2).

**This forces a qualification of one of this paper's own headline numbers.** The gap=a+bln K law with R^2=0.970 is measured along a single row of this grid, at AUROC_0=0.80. Across the grid as a whole ln K *alone* explains essentially none of the variance (R^2=0.038, and at LOO -0.192 worse than predicting the mean), so the K-law is real *within* an operating point and not a description of severity in general — the table shows a 4–7x span across K where there is headroom and essentially none at AUROC_0=0.985. This qualification is *stronger* on the corrected grid than on the degenerate one it replaces (R^2=0.038 against 0.114), so unlike the interaction claim it does not depend on the removed cells. Candidate count is a second-order modifier of a first-order operating-point effect, not a co-equal axis.

**What this fit does not license.** It is a surface fit inside one synthetic harness with one mechanism held fixed at one fixed n_val, and it is an average, not an upper bound, so it cannot certify that any particular pipeline's inflation is below anything. It also does not transport across harnesses: §5.4 shows two real-feature harnesses exceeding this surface's own operating-point relationship by 14–52x at a matched operating point. We report it because the review was right that the attempt was missing, and name a formally-derived bound as future work rather than claim this is one. **The out-of-sample check is also weaker than the previous version of this paper presented it as**, on two counts found by instrumenting rather than by argument — two of the five held-out Sweep A cells are themselves degenerate, for exactly the reason K=3 was dropped, and Sweep A runs 200 seeds against this grid's 100, which at these effect sizes matters more than the models do — so we read nothing into the resulting ordering, and this harness does not currently support an out-of-sample test of the joint surface (Appendix C.3).

### 5.6 Why this matters practically, and what it does not establish

The operating-point relationship explains, without appeal to mechanism identity, why several severity estimates in this paper come out near zero: they were measured on tasks already running at AUROC >=0.98, where there is almost no headroom for selection to inflate anything. That includes the 12 ceiling-saturated cells of Case Study 4 (+0.0042, against +0.0157 on the non-saturated cells whose estimator is non-degenerate — a 3.7x difference), MultiHaluDet's own 0.985 regime, and this paper's own real-feature harness before it was recalibrated. A reader auditing a pipeline should therefore ask *how many candidates were tried* and *where on the difficulty curve the reported number sits*, not primarily *which of the five mechanisms* is present — while still using the mechanism taxonomy to know where to look.

**What this does not establish.** The three sweeps vary one quantity at a time and the joint grid varies two, all within a single synthetic harness holding the mechanism fixed; none is a matched head-to-head across all five mechanisms at common (K, n_val, AUROC_0), which would be needed to test whether mechanism identity explains any *residual* variance once these are controlled. We do not claim it explains none, and that experiment is not run here. The joint grid narrows the gap — it establishes the *scale* on which the two quantities combine, which a mechanism-level comparison would have to hold constant — but does not close it, and varies n_val not at all. **And §5.4 sharpens what that missing experiment has to be:** two of the three harnesses compared there differ in mechanic as well as in harness, and exceed the synthetic surface's prediction by more than an order of magnitude at a matched operating point. That is not evidence that mechanism identity matters — harness and mechanic are confounded in exactly those rows — but it does mean the one-quantity-at-a-time result can no longer be read as showing mechanism identity unimportant *in general*, only unimportant relative to K and the operating point *within* a single harness. Disentangling the two is now the highest-value experiment this line of work has open.

## 6. A Checklist for This Literature

[Full version in `draft/leakage_checklist.md`.] Five questions correspond to the five mechanisms above:

1. Is any feature computed by fitting something on the full dataset's labels before an outer CV loop treats part of that dataset as held out?

2. Inside a CV fold, is a "best" checkpoint/epoch chosen using the same fold whose features/predictions get reused downstream as clean OOF output?

3. Is a "best" layer/config selected by the argmax of a metric computed on the same test set later reported as the headline number, across many candidates, with no correction?

4. Was a hyperparameter chosen by maximizing a cross-validated metric, with that same CV number then reported as performance, with no further genuinely held-out check?

5. Was a decision threshold or other operating point chosen by optimizing a metric on the test labels themselves, with that same test set then used to report the metric at that threshold?

### 6.2 We tried to automate this checklist; the attempt itself is informative

We built a regex-based scanner for the original four patterns (Mechanism 5 was found after it was built and is not covered) and ran it against 7 repositories: the two already-audited case studies, to test whether it catches known bugs, plus 5 not-previously-examined, externally-found hidden-state-probing repositories, to test its false-positive rate on presumed-clean pipelines (`code/04`, `results/leakage_linter_report.json`). It is a heuristic line-matcher, not a validated static analyzer; every flag was manually read and classified before being treated as a finding.

**How the hits were classified, and the reliability limits of that classification.** The true-positive/false-positive labels were originally a single rater's manual reading; that rater was this paper's author; and the rater already knew where both real bugs were — no inter-rater reliability, no blinding, an interested rater, and none of it disclosed. We state it, and obtained an independent second reading of the same 7 sites under a protocol withholding this paper's draft, its checklist and the location of the known bugs. **That second rater was a language model, not a second human**, which bounds what the agreement is worth: it checks that the classification survives a reader with no stake in the result and no knowledge of the answer, not that two domain experts converge. Agreement was 7 of 7, every site a false positive on the same provenance argument, so percent agreement is 100% and *Cohen's kappa is undefined here* on a single-category marginal; seven non-independent sites from two repositories is a weak reliability check, not a strong one.

The same blind read cut in three further directions, detailed in Appendix D.2. It located both known true positives without being told where they were — direct evidence that the two false negatives below are failures of the *scanner* rather than artifacts of the authors' privileged knowledge. It declined to count MultiHaluDet's checkpoint selection (Mechanism 3) as qualifying, calling it "a mild stacking impurity" instead; we disagree, but the disagreement is genuine, is about whether a site clears a binary threshold rather than about what the code does, and is exactly the kind of judgement a single non-blinded rater cannot surface. And it surfaced two sites in `HallucinationPatternDetection` we had not previously reported and have since verified against the pinned commit — further instances of Mechanisms 4 and 5, the second establishing that Mechanism 5 is not specific to MultiHaluDet (§4.5). Neither changes any severity number here, and neither is included in the 7-hit count below.

**The scanner does not work, for two specific reasons.** It produced 7 raw hits, concentrated in 2 repos, and manual reading showed all 7 are false positives: 3 flag t-SNE/UMAP/PCA calls used only for plotting; 4 flag `haloscope`'s layer/threshold selection, where reading the code shows selection correctly uses a separate validation split — the opposite of leakage, and a positive data point for that NeurIPS 2024 pipeline. More tellingly it missed both known true positives, and **both misses have identifiable causes in its own construction:** a plain keyword-list gap at Case Study 3's site, and at Case Study 4's a design choice to require an argmax token and a `test`-named variable on the same line and in the same file, which cannot fire there however the keyword lists are extended because the test split lives one call away in another module. Zero true positives among the 5 newly-scanned repos, two false negatives on the two known positives, seven-for-seven false positives on its raw hits: this particular scanner does not work. We deliberately do not read that as evidence that the leakage class is inherently resistant to static detection — the first cause needs a longer keyword list, and the second points at a specific, tractable design requirement, connecting the argmax to the test-derived metric across statements and function boundaries by dataflow analysis rather than line matching. What the exercise establishes is narrower and more useful: *line-level regex matching* is the wrong tool here, because every one of these mechanisms is a relationship between a selection statement and the provenance of the quantity it selects on, and that relationship is routinely split across lines, functions and files (Appendix D.3).

This is a statement about 2 of 7 repositories found by targeted search for exactly this failure class, not a random or exhaustive sample of the field (HaRP and GUARDIAN, this paper's own pipelines, were never scanned at all); the population-level prevalence of any of these five mechanisms remains unknown and is not estimated by this count. **On what a non-regex approach can do, and why we do not generalize from that either:** the blinded second-rater protocol above is incidentally an instance of one — a language model reading the vendored source with no access to this paper — and it located both true positives, plus the two additional sites, where the regex scanner found none. That is one run, on two repositories already known to contain bugs, with no false-positive rate measured on the five presumed-clean ones: enough to say the failure above is *this scanner's* rather than automated detection's as a category, which is all §6 claims, and nowhere near enough to say a language-model scanner works. Measuring that properly, on a pre-registered corpus with both classes present, and building and validating a better analyzer, is follow-on work this paper does not do (Appendix D.4).

## 7. Discussion

**Data and code availability.** All code, cached result JSONs and the paper source ship in the anonymized supplementary material, and will be released publicly upon publication. Case Studies 2, 3 and 4 are reproducible from what ships here, on the terms §4 grades them against; **Case Study 1 is the exception, and no script, log or data file supporting its +0.19 figure is included.** The §6 regex linter ships in full and is, per its own results, a confirmed non-detector on this corpus (0 true positives, 2 false negatives) — included for transparency, not as a working tool a reader should rely on today.

**Scope, and what these numbers mean operationally.** This is an evaluation-protocol audit, not an inference-time method, so fixing any of the five mechanisms makes no claimed difference to deployment cost; the value is entirely in correcting reported detection metrics. A detector deployed at a reported AUROC above its true value will, at a fixed threshold, pass more hallucinations to production and flag more correct outputs for unnecessary review than promised — but the code-verified inflations measured here are on the order of 0.002 to 0.034 AUROC, not the tens of points an earlier framing of this work implied. Practitioners should read this as: these mechanisms are real and worth fixing, because they are cheap to fix and because they corrupt comparisons between methods, but not as an argument that the subfield's reported numbers are broadly wrong by large margins. We lack deployment-volume and review-cost data for any audited system, so we attempt no cost estimate. Where large gaps *do* appear (Mechanism 5's +0.021 to +0.034), they are on threshold-dependent metrics, not AUROC, and are not directly comparable to the AUROC-scale numbers. The audit's central lesson recurs in related, independently-conducted work on adjacent areas of LLM evaluation, none sharing a leakage mechanism with the four case studies here: an initially plausible result substantially weakens or changes character once a specific confound is directly tested rather than merely acknowledged as possible.

**Limitations.** *(1) Case Study 1 is not re-verifiable from this artifact* — no supporting script, log or data ships with the paper, and its +0.19 figure is excluded from every quantitative claim here. *(2) The central severity relationship (§5) is measured one-quantity-at-a-time in a single synthetic harness with the mechanism held fixed.* It shows that K and the operating point move severity a great deal; it does not show that mechanism identity explains *no* residual variance once those are controlled. A matched (K, n_val, AUROC_0) head-to-head across all five mechanisms in one harness would test that directly and is not run here. *(2a) We do not offer a formally-derived bound, and this is a real gap rather than an omission for space.* A function phi(K, n_sel, AUROC_0) upper-bounding mathbbE[AUROC_argmax-AUROC(l^star)], decreasing in n_sel and AUROC_0, would unify all five mechanisms and turn §6's checklist into a calculator; §5.5 reports an *empirical joint fit* instead, which is not a bound and which we never call phi. One obstacle is specific rather than generic: this harness's isolated n_val dependence runs *opposite* to the 1/sqrtn_sel direction such a derivation would predict (§5.3, Sweep D). *A second obstacle previously listed here is withdrawn:* we had also cited the extreme-value functional form as falsified here, but that test used a misspecified sigma and is uninformative in either direction — a measurement gap, closed by a harness instrumented to produce sigma across independent, exchangeable candidate evaluations. *(3) The extreme-value account of K-scaling is therefore neither confirmed nor refuted by this paper:* we withdraw the previous version's "falsified" language, report an empirical logarithmic regularity instead, and have no mechanistic explanation for it (Appendix A.11). *(4) The alternative-confound control for Mechanism 3 retains only sim26–37% of honest selection's placebo-relative benefit* and therefore has limited power (§4.3). *(4a) The fixed-d eigenspectrum experiment separates covariance shape from dimensionality but not from operating point*, because anisotropy lowers the achieved operating point even at a fixed Bayes-optimal J; separating them would need a generator holding the *achieved* operating point fixed, i.e. a calibration search per cell. *(4b) The measured severity of Mechanism 3 depends on a downstream ensembling step:* removing the cross-model feature averaging raises the gap by 2.3–5.8x, so the primary estimate is of inflation *in what the audited pipeline reports*, not of the mechanism's severity without that averaging. *(4c) Two arms of the fidelity extension differ in training depth (47.6%) as well as in validation signal* — reported rather than equalized, because when to stop *is* the leaked decision — and Mechanism 5's F1/accuracy gaps are measured at n_test=140 and fall by 6.2x at n_test=2000 (§4.5). *(4d) The scanner's classification rests on two raters, not a validated protocol* (§6): the primary rater is this paper's author and knew the ground truth, and while a second, blinded rater agreed 7/7, Cohen's kappa is undefined on a single-category marginal and seven non-independent sites from two repositories is a weak reliability check. *(5)* We could not re-run either external paper's full 7B-scale pipeline within this paper's compute budget, so Case Studies 3 and 4's severity for the *actual published numbers* remains an open question; our reconstruction of Case Study 3 supports a small, positive estimate at 2 of 4 tested capacities individually (1 of 4 after Holm-Bonferroni), and MultiHaluDet's actual architecture (6 transformer layers, multi-scale, heavy augmentation) is more expressive still, so this should not be over-read in either direction for their pipeline. The real-feature test is also single-dataset and single-language (n=400 HaluEval English `qa_samples` only), despite MultiHaluDet's own contribution being explicitly multilingual; and even leak-free, no calibration of this feature set's between-class mean, at any strength, brings it onto the synthetic sweep's exact 0.80 operating point, because a real portion of its discriminability lives in within-class structure the calibration cannot touch — so its severity estimate, while now trustworthy, is reported at its own (~0.94-0.95) operating point and the two are not numerically comparable. Whether the synthetic estimate transfers to real hidden states at the *same* operating point — on this dataset, non-English datasets, or larger ones — remains untested rather than confirmed or excluded.

## 8. Broader Impact and Ethical Considerations

This paper adds no detection capability; its subject is the trustworthiness of reported detector performance, so the foreseeable harm pathway runs through misplaced confidence rather than through misuse. A detector whose reported AUROC or F1 is inflated by any of the five mechanisms here will, at a fixed threshold, pass more hallucinations and flag more correct outputs than its published numbers promise — and a practitioner who carries such a number into a higher-stakes setting, such as clinical or legal document generation or automated monitoring of a model's factual claims, imports that gap into decisions where the cost of a missed hallucination and the cost of a false flag are not symmetric. **The taxonomy and checklist here are a partial mitigation and explicitly not a guarantee, and this paper's own evidence is why we say so:** two of our own measurement instruments were found committing the exact pattern we audit for (§4.3, Appendix A issues 8 and 13), four separate rounds of adversarial review were each needed to remove a different confound from a single case study (Appendix A), and Mechanism 5 was found only after the checklist and its scanner had already been written and did not cover it (§4.5, §6). Working through §6 raises the chance a leak is caught; it does not establish that a pipeline is clean, and nothing measured here would support reporting that it does. The severity numbers cut in the opposite direction and should be read with the same care: the code-verified AUROC-scale estimates land between roughly 0.000 and 0.034, which is neither a licence to ignore these mechanisms — they are cheap to fix and they corrupt method comparisons — nor a measurement of what they cost in any deployed system. Those figures come from specific synthetic harnesses and two audited third-party pipelines, at operating points and test-set sizes we chose or inherited; §5.4 shows the operating-point relationship governing them does not transport even to this paper's own real-feature harnesses (14–52x at a matched operating point), and Mechanism 5's F1 gap falls 6.2x between n_test=140 and 2000. Severity in a system that is not one of these has to be measured there rather than read off this paper.

## 9. Conclusion

Five distinct label-information-leakage mechanisms recur across hidden-state hallucination-detection pipelines, including our own. Three of the five are verified directly against pinned commits of externally published code. That taxonomy is this paper's most durable contribution, and we expect it to outlast every number in it.

**The severities, however, do not differ sharply — and an earlier version of this work was wrong to say they did.** Measured against matched controls, every mechanism here with a code-verified AUROC-scale estimate lands between roughly 0.000 and 0.034 AUROC: Mechanism 2's selection-specific component +0.0255 (SD 0.0186) under 50 randomized splits; Mechanism 3's checkpoint-selection severity +0.0011 to +0.0036 on the isotropic synthetic sweep, individually significant at 2 of 4 tested capacities and at 1 of 4 after Holm-Bonferroni; Mechanism 4's +0.0076 (BCa 95% CI [+0.0048,+0.0134]) across the 17 of 24 published result files on which its estimator is non-degenerate; Mechanism 5's effect on AUROC exactly zero by construction, against F1/accuracy gaps of +0.021 to +0.034 that are large and highly significant on their own terms though not directly comparable in magnitude to the AUROC-scale numbers — a clear case that a pipeline can be simultaneously clean on one reported metric and leaking on another. Case Study 1's much larger +0.19 figure is reported from prior work and is *not* re-verifiable from this artifact, so we exclude it from that comparison rather than let it carry the claim.

**What severity does track is continuous and mechanism-independent.** Holding the mechanism fixed and varying only the number of candidates selected among moves the gap monotonically, well fit by a+bln K (R^2=0.970); varying only the operating point moves it by 48.6x, from +0.0093 at AUROC_0=0.70 to +0.0002 at 0.985 (§5). Both effects are larger than any difference we measure between mechanisms *inside that harness* — though §5.4 shows the operating-point relationship does not transport outside it, exceeded by 14–52x at a matched operating point in this paper's own real-feature harnesses, so it is a within-harness severity modifier and not a general law. A KxAUROC_0 factorial fitting the two jointly shows the axes are not co-equal: across that grid ln K alone explains R^2=0.038, while a separable multiplicative empirical fit describes the surface well (R^2=0.976), so candidate count is a second-order modifier of a first-order operating-point effect. Two claims of earlier versions are withdrawn rather than restated: that the two axes *interact*, which came entirely from a degenerate K=3 column in which the leaky and control arms are bitwise identical in most folds; and that the extreme-value form c sigma_valsqrt(2ln K) is falsified, which the only sigma this harness produces — a single training trajectory's dispersion — cannot establish either way (Appendix A.11). The empirical a+bln K law never references sigma and is unaffected.

The difficulty of establishing any of these numbers — needing calibration, budget-matching, seed-matching, an alternative-confound test and a leak-free measurement instrument all to hold at once — is this paper's strongest argument for the checklist we provide, more so than any single mechanism in isolation. That checklist lets researchers and reviewers ask precise, mechanism-specific questions of a pipeline's evaluation protocol, replacing "did you use cross-validation correctly" as a single, undifferentiated concern. The automated-scanner exercise (§6) covers seven repositories, too small a sample to support any claim about how common these five mechanisms are across the wider literature, and we make none. A pre-registered audit applying this checklist to a fixed sample of 15-20 externally published probing papers, selected before any of them are examined, is planned as follow-on work; that protocol is already drafted.

- **11.** Two of this paper's own synthetic-data generators (`code/46`, Mechanism 5; `code/47`, the K/selection-set-size sweep) independently committed the same calibration-inversion error class as issue 2 above, in a new form: the per-class mean offset used the *full* separation on each side (+/-class_sep) rather than half (+/-class_sep/2), silently realizing 4x the intended Fisher J and mislabeling every operating point in both scripts' output (e.g. a labeled 0.985-AUROC cell actually realized Bayes-optimal AUROC ~0.99994). *Fix:* corrected both generators to the +/-class_sep/2 convention already used elsewhere in this paper (`code/02d`), and added a new, non-tautological guardrail (`code/sanity_checks.py`).

*Three limits on that guardrail, disclosed rather than left implicit.* *(a) Coverage.* Despite its docstring's stated intent it is currently called only by the two generators that needed it (`code/46`, `code/47`); the pre-existing generators (`code/02d`, `22`, `27`, and others) were not retrofitted, so "every synthetic generator in this project must call it" is aspirational, not yet true. *(b) Applicability.* For `code/27` the guardrail is not merely un-retrofitted but not *applicable*: its bias correction hat J=lVertbar x_+-bar x_-rVert^2-d(1/n_++1/n_-) is exact only under identity within-class covariance Sigma=I, since the subtracted term equals d(1/n_++1/n_-) only when every dimension has unit variance and dimensions are uncorrelated. Under a general Sigma the correct term is operatornametr(Sigma)(1/n_++1/n_-) and the target must be the Mahalanobis Deltamu^->pSigma^-1Deltamu, so calling it on `code/27`'s real, correlated covariance could both reject correct data and pass buggy data; making it applicable requires modifying the estimator, not adding a call site, and the assumption is now stated in `sanity_checks.py`'s docstring. The generators it does gate are isotropic by construction, so the assumption holds where it is used; the anisotropic generators (`code/27`, `code/56`) verify calibration by a different route, and `code/56` can do so exactly because it constructs Sigma rather than estimating it. *(c) Sensitivity.* The guardrail's own design needed two iterations: fitting a regularized classifier and checking its CV AUROC against the target systematically undershoots by sim0.04-0.05 AUROC (a real, separately-documented regularization effect, not a bug – see issue 5), so it instead checks the bias-corrected realized Fisher J ratio directly, which cleanly separates correct generators (ratio 0.33-1.83 across 2000 seeds and five operating points) from the 4x bug (ratio never below 2.77). At small n_samples and low target AUROC (verified at n_samples=350, target 0.70) the two distributions begin to overlap (correct-generator ratios reach 2.32, buggy ones fall as low as 2.03), so the bounds ([0.2,2.6]) were widened to prioritize never rejecting correct data, at the cost of sensitivity in exactly that regime — which is the regime of `code/47`'s Sweep B cell at n_samples=350. A reader should treat that cell as somewhat less protected against this bug class than the others.

The audited repo does not: `trainer.py:76` constructs `ReduceLROnPlateau` with its own `patience=3`, while the early-stopping break at `trainer.py:149` tests against `config.patience = 15` (`config.py:19`). Breaking after 3 non-improving epochs truncates every run drastically. *Fix:* separate counters, 3 for the scheduler and 15 for early stopping.

- **13.** **The train-only calibration fix from issue 8 was itself still label-conditional.** Estimating class means from train indices fixes estimation, not application: `mask = y == cls` ranges over the whole array, so each test point's own label still decided which class offset was subtracted from it – a residual instance of Mechanism 1, in this paper's own instrument, for the second time in the same script. Its signature: at alpha=0, where the classes should be exactly indistinguishable, that transform still leaves train-fit -> test-eval AUROC =0.584+/-0.059. *Fix:* `apply_calibration_label_free` (`code/43`) — axis u, midpoint c and pooled within-class SD s_w estimated from train only, then the identical label-free map pmapstoalpha p+sqrt(1-alpha^2)s_wvarepsilon applied to every row. Calling the result a "fidelity extension" while differing on all of that was an overclaim. *Fix:* all of the above are ported, and the module docstring now carries an exhaustive ported/not-ported list rather than an implicit claim. §4.3 names what remains unported — the objective, the weight EMA, the augmentation, and the model class — so the honest scope is fidelity of the *validation-signal coupling and optimizer schedule*, not of the objective or the architecture.

*What it moved.* The reported gap rises from +0.0221 to +0.0338, the like-for-like ratio against the checkpoint-selection-only harness from 2.4x to 3.6x, and — helpfully — the extension's LEAKY operating point from 0.9176 to 0.9424 (see item 12). It also *costs* this appendix a claim: the 2x2 ablation's sign flip (item 12) does not survive the port and is retracted there. A separate, smaller bug found in the same pass: `code/49` hardcoded its output JSON's `calibration_method` field to `label_free_axis_noising` even in the ablation cells that ran the superseded transform, mislabelling two of the four result files; the field now reports whichever transform actually ran.

**Issue 8 is the load-bearing finding of this appendix**, and the reason this paper's own severity-measurement instrument is used as a worked example of Mechanism 1 in §4.3: a full-dataset, label-dependent transform applied before the split it is later evaluated on – the exact pattern this paper's checklist tells readers to look for in other people's code, found instead in ours.

**What this correction history demonstrates about itself.** A single confirmatory- or null-looking number was not enough at any step above – a correctly-inverted formula, a budget/seed-matched control, adequate power, an unbiased calibration estimator, and a train-only split all had to hold *simultaneously*, and each in turn was checked, found wanting, and fixed by a fresh round of review. The pre-registered decision rule (`GENUINE_LEAK_CONFIRMED` / `CONFOUND_CONFIRMED_NO_REAL_LEAK` / `MIXED`, from `code/02d`) returns `MIXED` on most tested versions of this experiment, `CONFOUND_CONFIRMED_NO_REAL_LEAK` on the one ceiling-confounded version, and `GENUINE_LEAK_CONFIRMED` on the current, label-free-calibrated real-feature test – an earlier claim that the confound branch was structurally unreachable was itself wrong, and is corrected here. All three branches are reachable, none is stable across configurations, and the verdict moved toward our own hypothesis when the calibration was corrected – which is exactly when a single pre-registered label deserves least weight. This is why §4.3's claims rest on effect sizes with intervals, not on the rule's output.

### A.2 The primary sweep: control design and the randomization fix

**A second, smaller asymmetry inside CLEAN_MATCHED, disclosed at a review's request.** CLEAN_MATCHED's epoch count is *selected* by a model trained on only 85% of the fold's training data — the 15% carve-out it selects against is taken out of that data (`tr2_idx` versus `es_idx` in `code/47`) — but the retrain that produces the reported model then uses 100% of it (`tr_idx`), while LEAKY's epoch count is selected by a model trained on the full 100%. So the two arms are matched on the retrain's *data* budget, which is what this control was built for, but not on the data budget of the run the epoch count was chosen from. Its direction is not determined a priori: a model trained on less data typically peaks later, which would push CLEAN_MATCHED's chosen epoch up, but it is also a noisier selection signal. We disclose rather than correct it, because removing it would require selecting on a carve-out drawn from outside the fold, which changes what the control is a control for.

**This table changed under a randomization fix, and the change is instructive rather than cosmetic.** An independent review found that `code/02d` — the harness behind this table — passed *the replicate index i itself* to every randomization point in the replicate: the generator seed for the synthetic data draw, the `random_state` of the outer `train_test_split`, and the `random_state` of the inner `StratifiedKFold`. The defect was one integer aliased across three independent randomization points — `seeds_for(i)` returned `(i, i, i, i)` — not three seed streams that had become correlated; there was never more than one stream, so across the 100 replicates those sources of variation were perfectly confounded. This is exactly the confound `code/47`'s explicitly decoupled `data_seed`/`split_seed`/`fold_seed_base`/ `init_seed_base` scheme was written to fix, and it had never been retrofitted to `code/02d`. It is now (`seeds_for()`; the previous behaviour is reproducible bit-for-bit via `–coupled-seed` and its output ships as `results/corrected_capacity_placebo_sweep_coupled_seed_legacy.json`, so the comparison below is checkable rather than asserted). §4.3's table is the rerun.

**The magnitudes are stable; which capacities clear significance is not.** The band moves from +0.0009–+0.0034 to +0.0011–+0.0036 — a shift of a few ten-thousandths of an AUROC point, well inside these cells' seed-draw noise — but capacity 48 goes from p=0.015 to p=0.171 and capacity 16 goes the other way, from p=0.309 to p=0.019. We state this plainly because it is weaker than the previous, seed-confounded version of this table, under which two capacities survived Holm-Bonferroni: the randomization fix cost this result one surviving cell. That the significance pattern is this movable across a change which leaves the effect sizes essentially fixed is itself the point, and the reason this paper rests on effect sizes with intervals rather than on per-cell significance labels. As an internal check that this is the fix rather than a new bug, the decoupled capacity-128 cell (+0.0036, p=0.0012) now reproduces `code/47`'s default cell (+0.0036, p=0.0012), the identical configuration, which had used decoupled seeds all along and differed from `code/02d` under the coupled scheme. *We previously called that cell "independently-written" and withdraw it:* `code/47`'s own docstring describes its generative process and LEAKY/CLEAN/CLEAN_MATCHED/PLACEBO logic as "an exact port of `code/02d`," so the two agreeing is a *determinism check*, not independent confirmation by a second implementation. No such independent implementation exists in this paper. `code/55`'s readouts were retrofitted with the identical seed streams and rerun; the other synthetic generators (`code/22`, `code/27`) still use the coupled scheme and were not, being robustness checks reported against their own baselines rather than contributors to this table.

**Intervals, multiplicity and power for the primary table.** BCa bootstrap 95% CIs on that gap (`code/44`, 10,000 resamples, paired permutation p alongside Wilcoxon), at capacities 16/48/128/384: [+0.0007,+0.0036] (p=0.009); [-0.0004,+0.0028] (p=0.175); [+0.0019,+0.0057] (p=0.0003); [-0.0002,+0.0052] (p=0.091) — concordant with the Wilcoxon verdicts above.

CLEAN_MATCHED beats PLACEBO decisively at every capacity (p<10^-6 throughout, gaps +0.012 to +0.025), confirming honest checkpoint selection is itself strongly informative. The LEAKY-CLEAN_MATCHED residual – the actual leakage estimate – is individually significant at 2 of the 4 capacities (16 and 128, p=0.019 and p=0.0012), but **only capacity 128 survives Holm-Bonferroni correction across the four-capacity family** (0.0012x4=0.0049, against 0.019x3=0.058 for the next-smallest). The two non-significant cells are underpowered rather than null: their observed +0.0011 and +0.0024 sit below the exact per-seed MDEs of 0.0023 and 0.0039 (alpha=0.05, 80% power, n=100). The correction is applied within this four-capacity family only.

### A.3 Alternative explanation 1: is it adaptivity itself?

**Is it "having adaptive checkpoint selection at all," rather than fold reuse?** CLEAN_MATCHED's full-budget retrain is a blind, fixed-epoch run rather than an adaptively-selected one, so an independent review proposed that LEAKY's advantage might reflect adaptivity itself. We tested this with a fifth condition, CLEAN_MATCHED_ADAPTIVE: full training budget and *ongoing* adaptive checkpoint selection during that same run – the identical mechanism LEAKY uses – but selecting against the disjoint, honestly held-out carve-out instead of the reused fold (`code/22_epoch_forcing_confound_control.py`). If "having adaptive selection" were the true driver, CLEAN_MATCHED_ADAPTIVE should approach LEAKY. It does not: at capacity 128, LEAKY beats CLEAN_MATCHED_ADAPTIVE by +0.0198 (p<0.0001) – larger than the original LEAKY-CLEAN_MATCHED gap, not smaller – and CLEAN_MATCHED_ADAPTIVE itself underperforms the blind CLEAN_MATCHED control (-0.0164, p<0.0001). At capacity 384 the same pattern holds and is significant (+0.0143, p<0.0001), where the original comparison was only borderline.

**How much this control can actually settle.** Its own power is limited, and we quantify that rather than assert it. On the scale that matters here — how much of honest checkpoint selection's benefit over the placebo the control still retains, (CLEAN_MATCHED_ADAPTIVE - PLACEBO)/(CLEAN_MATCHED - PLACEBO), from `results/epoch_forcing_confound_control.json` — it retains only 37.3% at capacity 128 (+0.0098 of +0.0262) and 25.7% at capacity 384 (+0.0040 of +0.0156). A condition that has lost roughly two-thirds to three-quarters of the effect it was supposed to hold constant is not a sharp instrument. So: this test retains only sim26–37% of honest selection's placebo-relative benefit, is directionally inconsistent with the "any adaptive selection helps" alternative, and we do not treat it as having ruled that alternative out. CLEAN_MATCHED_ADAPTIVE's own underperformance against the blind control is itself unexpected; the most likely account is that adaptive selection against a small carve-out is a noisy selection signal, a different phenomenon from the fold-reuse question. **That carve-out is also a genuine confound with the LEAKY vs. CLEAN_MATCHED_ADAPTIVE comparison itself**: LEAKY selects against its full 112-sample validation fold (560/5), while CLEAN_MATCHED_ADAPTIVE selects against only a 67-sample carve-out (15% of the 448-sample training split) – a 1.67x difference in selection-set size, whose noise would inflate, not just accompany, the gap this control reports. A cleaner version would size the carve-out to match the fold (25% of 448=112); we did not rerun it at that setting, and flag this as an open robustness gap.

### A.4 Alternative explanation 2: downstream feature averaging

**Is the measured severity small only because the pipeline averages it away?** The primary harness (§4.3) — like MultiHaluDet's own `run_pipeline.py:108` that it mirrors — accumulates each fold-model's test-set activations and divides by K before the meta-learner scores them. A review pointed out that this averages the activations of five independently-initialized models, so if the leaked signal is fold-specific it could be largely cancelled before the reported metric ever sees it: the mechanism would not be benign, the instrument would be destroying the effect. The obvious version of the test, "rerun with K=1," is not runnable, and not for budget reasons: with one fold there is no out-of-fold partition, so no OOF features exist to train the meta-learner on. `code/55` instead reads out the *same trained fold-models* three ways — averaged (the shipped pipeline), un-averaged (the meta-learner scores each fold-model's own test features and the K AUROCs are averaged after scoring, so no activation is ever mixed), and the OOF matrix read directly.

**The alternative explanation is supported, and we report it as such.** Removing the averaging raises the LEAKY-CLEAN_MATCHED gap at every capacity, by roughly 2.3–5.8x: +0.0073/+0.0066/+0.0084/+0.0084 un-averaged at capacities 16/48/128/384, against +0.0019/+0.0011/+0.0036/+0.0024 averaged — and it is significant at all four capacities un-averaged, against two of four averaged. Reading the OOF matrix directly agrees (+0.0058/+0.0096/+0.0086/+0.0042, significant at all four). A diagnostic confirms the premise: cross-validating the meta-learner *across* the OOF matrix, training on rows from some fold-models and scoring rows from others, sits at chance (0.484–0.522 for every condition and capacity), i.e. the fold-models' feature spaces carry essentially no information about one another, so averaging their activations really is mixing incompatible bases. We keep both readings.

The *averaged* number is the correct estimate of inflation in what the audited pipeline actually reports, because averaging is what that pipeline does — so §4.3's table stands unchanged as this paper's primary estimate. The *un-averaged* number is the correct estimate of the mechanism's severity at its source, and it is about 3x larger. The honest summary is therefore narrower than "this mechanism is small": it is small *as reported*, partly because a downstream ensembling step attenuates it, and a pipeline with the same leak but without that averaging step should be expected to show more. Full results: `results/oof_averaging_control.json`.

### A.5 A second generative process: anisotropic covariance

**A second, structurally different generative process – with two disclosed confounds of its own.** §4.3's primary sweep draws each class from an isotropic Gaussian, so a toy-model artifact is a live alternative explanation. We built a second generative process that keeps the controlled sweep design but replaces the covariance structure: fit the real pooled within-class covariance and mean-difference direction from the real Mistral-7B/HaluEval features used in §4.3, then rescale the mean difference (keeping the real, anisotropic, correlated covariance shape exactly as observed) to hit the same AUROC=0.80 target via the binormal identity (`code/27_anisotropic_covariance_capacity_sweep.py`, n=100 seeds, same four capacities). Two confounds limit how cleanly this isolates covariance shape: the feature dimensionality changes from 64 (isotropic) to 414 (real features' native dimension), a 6.5x change in the dimension-to-sample ratio that itself affects checkpoint-selection dynamics; and the realized empirical AUROC under the trained MLP is 0.672-0.681 across capacities, not the 0.753-0.765 the isotropic sweep achieves despite both targeting the same Bayes-optimal 0.80 — so some of the gap difference between them is not attributable to covariance shape alone.

With those disclosed, the pattern: LEAKY vs. CLEAN_MATCHED is +0.0082 (p=0.007, capacity 128, vs. +0.0036 isotropic) and +0.0099 (p=0.009, capacity 384, vs. +0.0024, not significant, isotropic), while at the two lower capacities it weakens (capacity 16: +0.0000, p=0.85; capacity 48: +0.0016, p=0.22) and CLEAN_MATCHED vs. PLACEBO becomes significant instead at those same two (+0.0084, p=0.015; +0.0080, p=0.010). Reporting all four capacities for that second comparison, not only where it is significant: capacity 128 is +0.0024 (p=0.45) and capacity 384 *reverses sign* to -0.0037 (p=0.11), neither significant — permuted-label checkpoint selection nominally outperforming honest checkpoint selection, the same sign anomaly the epoch-count diagnostic (A.10) was built to explain and which does not recur under either corrected real-feature calibration. Only two of the four capacities (128 significant in both, 48 non-significant in both) are significance-concordant between the sweeps, so the capacity-by-capacity pattern reshuffles rather than replicating cleanly.

Pooling all eight synthetic LEAKY-vs-CLEAN_MATCHED tests (both sweeps) into one Holm-Bonferroni family, rather than treating the anisotropic sweep as a separate robustness check, leaves *two* of the eight significant at the family-wise 0.05 level — both capacity-128 cells (0.0012x8=0.0099 and 0.0071x7=0.049), with the procedure stopping at the anisotropic capacity-384 cell (0.0087x6=0.052). *This verdict moved in our own favour* when the seed-decoupling fix landed: under the previous, seed-confounded isotropic run the same pooled family retained nothing at all (0.007x8=0.056). We flag the direction of that movement explicitly — a result that improves when its own instrument is corrected deserves the most scrutiny, not the least. What survives either way is the capacity-128 cell, in both generative processes. We report the anisotropic sweep's p-values uncorrected, in a robustness-check role, not as a second independent confirmation carrying equal weight to the pre-registered isotropic family. Taken together this is evidence against the isotropic-covariance assumption alone being responsible for the severity estimate, and does not shrink or eliminate the gap – but it is weaker, less clean evidence than a simple "replicates and is larger" summary would suggest.

### A.6 The discriminating experiment: a fixed-d eigenspectrum sweep

**The discriminating experiment: covariance shape or dimensionality?** The comparison in A.5 cannot say, because the two sweeps differ in both. An independent review named the experiment that separates them, and it is cheap: hold d=64 — the isotropic sweep's dimension — and vary *only* the within-class eigenvalue profile (`code/56`). We fix a single random orthonormal eigenbasis shared by every cell, so the eigen*basis* does not move either; renormalize every spectrum to operatornametr(Sigma)=d, so total within-class variance is identical everywhere and only its distribution across directions differs; use one fixed mean-difference direction with equal components in that eigenbasis, the neutral choice in that it does not preferentially align Deltamu with the high- or low-variance directions of any spectrum; and set its length per cell so the Mahalanobis J=Deltamu^->pSigma^-1Deltamu hits the same AUROC_0=0.80 target exactly (verified to 10^-9; checked directly rather than through `sanity_checks.py`, whose bias correction assumes Sigma=I — see A.1, issue 11). Cells: power-law spectra lambda_ipropto i^-beta for betain0,0.5,1,2, where beta=0 is an exact isotropic control, plus `real_top64`, the top-64 eigenvalues of the same real within-class covariance the d=414 sweep uses.

**Covariance shape does move severity at fixed d — but the experiment buys one separation at the cost of another.** At capacity 128 the gap rises monotonically with anisotropy: +0.0015 (beta=0, not significant), +0.0038, +0.0044, +0.0104 (beta=2), with `real_top64` at +0.0048 — a roughly 7x span driven by nothing but the eigenvalue profile. So the isotropic-vs-anisotropic difference above is *not* purely a dimensionality artifact, which is what this experiment was built to establish. **But anisotropy and the achieved operating point are perfectly rank-confounded in this design**, and unavoidably so: concentrating the within-class variance into fewer directions makes the task harder for the MLP even at a fixed Bayes-optimal J, so LEAKY falls from 0.7523 (beta=0) to 0.5826 (beta=2) across exactly these cells. At capacity 128 the gap is perfectly rank-inversely ordered by that operating point (Spearman -1.00 across all five cells) — which is §5's relationship again, and means these data cannot distinguish "anisotropy raises severity" from "anisotropy lowers the operating point, and a lower operating point raises severity." We do not claim to have distinguished them. **The ordering is also not stable across capacity**: at capacity 384 the same five cells give +0.0043/+0.0036/+0.0062/+0.0039 (n.s.)/+0.0056, a Spearman of -0.10 — no monotone pattern at all. Pooling all ten cells gives Spearman -0.73 (p=0.016). One calibration note that should temper how finely any of these are read: the beta=0 control is nominally the same configuration as `code/02d`'s capacity-128 cell but draws different samples, and gives +0.0015 (p=0.60) against `code/02d`'s +0.0036 (p=0.0012) — seed-draw noise at an effect size of a few thousandths of an AUROC point, and the reason these cells should be compared *within* `code/56` (against its own beta=0 control) rather than against `code/02d`. Full results: `results/eigenspectrum_sweep_fixed_dim.json`.

### A.7 Real-feature calibration, in full

Feeding real Mistral-7B/HaluEval features (n=400, MultiHaluDet's own unmodified feature-extraction code, a 4-bit AWQ checkpoint pinned to a fixed revision) through the same 5-fold out-of-fold-plus-meta-learner LEAKY/CLEAN_MATCHED/PLACEBO architecture used in §4.3 gives LEAKY beating CLEAN_MATCHED by only +0.0002 AUROC (p=0.56, n=100 seeds) at capacity 128 and +0.0001 (p=0.96) at capacity 384. **That comparison is not usable as reported: every condition operates at AUROC ~0.985 on these raw real features** – a task with no room to fail leaves no room for a leakage bug to show inflation either, and an absolute-AUROC-point MDE computed at a 0.80 operating point is not comparable to an effect measured at 0.985.

Calibrating the real features down to the synthetic sweep's operating point took four rounds of correction, two of which found this paper's own measurement instrument committing the very pattern it audits for. Issues 5, 6, 8, 9 and 13 of A.1 record the full progression; two findings matter to a reader of this section. *First*, the transform used to rescale the features centered class means on the *full* labeled sample before any train/test split existed. Because each class's per-sample deviations sum to exactly zero over the sample used to estimate the class means, any subsequent split mechanically forces the two halves' leftover class-mean directions into an algebraic identity (n_tr*overlineDelta_tr = -n_te*overlineDelta_te), verified directly at alpha=0: cos(Deltamu_train,Deltamu_test)=-0.9999, and a linear model fit on train and scored on test gives AUROC =0.06 — not "zero separation," but near-perfect, mechanically-induced anti-correlation. That is exactly the pattern this paper's own checklist names (§6). *Second*, the obvious fix — re-centering on train indices only (`code/43`) — removes the algebraic artifact (cos = 0.0000+/-0.0007, train-fit->test-eval AUROC =0.58+/-0.06) but is incomplete in precisely the way that makes it interesting: estimating the class means from train only fixes *estimation*, not *application*. The transform still decided which class offset to subtract from each point — test points included — by reading that point's own label (`mask = y == cls` ranges over the whole array). Its signature is visible: at alpha=0, where classes should be exactly indistinguishable, that transform still leaves a train-fit->test-eval AUROC of 0.584+/-0.059, not chance.

**The replacement: a fully label-free calibration.** It consults no per-sample label at all. From the train indices only we estimate the class-mean axis u, the midpoint c, and the pooled within-class SD along u, s_w; then every row of the matrix — train and test alike — is passed through the identical map pmapstoalpha p+sqrt1-alpha^2 s_wvarepsilon on its projection p=langle x-c,urangle, with varepsilonsimmathcalN(0,1) drawn independently. This shrinks the between-class separation along u by exactly alpha while preserving the marginal spread along u, so difficulty is monotone in alpha and reaches chance exactly at alpha=0 — which it does: 0.487+/-0.092, statistically indistinguishable from 0.5, versus the label-conditional version's residual 0.584. *A simpler label-free candidate does not work, and the negative result is load-bearing:* pure directional shrinkage toward the midpoint, xmapsto x-(1-alpha)langle x-c,urangle u, with no noise re-injection, consults no label — but for any alpha>0 it is an *invertible linear map*, so it cannot reduce linear separability at all: it rescales the discriminative axis and the within-class spread along that axis by the identical factor, leaving d' unchanged. Measured behaviour confirms this: AUROC 0.993 at alpha=0.2031 where the label-free axis-noising transform gives 0.671. Both transforms are retained in `code/43`'s diagnostic so this rejection is reproducible rather than asserted.

**Tie disclosure for these Wilcoxon tests.** The paired differences contain many tied absolute values — 33 of 100 at capacity 128 and 40 of 100 at capacity 384 (plus 1 and 2 exact zeros respectively), because AUROC on an 80-sample test split takes discrete values. The Wilcoxon signed-rank statistic degrades under ties, so we report the paired sign-flip permutation test alongside it throughout (`code/44`); here both give the same verdict. This matters because under the *superseded* label-conditional calibration the tie counts were substantially worse (49 and 60 of 100) and the two tests disagreed by an order of magnitude in p (Wilcoxon p=0.00064 versus permutation p=0.0025 at capacity 128) — a fact the previous version of this section quoted the Wilcoxon p without disclosing.

### A.8 The fidelity extension: training depth and the scope of "fidelity"

**The two arms differ in training depth as well as in validation signal.** That depth is chosen by the same signal, so it is not an independent nuisance: LEAKY's kept checkpoint sits at a mean epoch of 29.14 against `CLEAN_MATCHED_PLUS_LRSCHED`'s 19.75, a gap of +9.40 epochs or +47.6% (n=100 seeds x 5 folds, Wilcoxon p=7.2x10^-13); the placebo arm, whose selection signal carries no information, stops earliest of all at 7.95. (An earlier version of this harness tracked this and lost the tracking when the training loop was re-ported; it is restored in `code/49`'s `best_epoch_stats`, and the difference under the fully-ported configuration is *larger*, not smaller, than the version that went missing.) So the +0.0338 is measured across arms that differ in training depth by roughly half, on top of the separately-disclosed ~15% data-budget difference (`tr2_idx` versus `tr_idx`, A.1 item 10). **We do not treat the depth difference as a nuisance to be subtracted**: choosing when to stop *is* the leaked decision this extension exists to model, and an arm forced to LEAKY's epoch count would no longer be reacting to its own honest signal — which is exactly the degenerate control A.1 item 10's second fix attempt had to back out of. But a reader should know the two arms are not matched on it, and that the depth ordering (LEAKY deepest, placebo shallowest) is itself the signature of a more informative selection signal producing later stopping points, not an artifact independent of the mechanism.

**Exactly what "fidelity" means here, and exactly what it does not.** A review found that this extension, while porting the validation-signal coupling faithfully, silently differed from the audited trainer on most of its optimizer and data-pipeline settings, which made "fidelity extension" an overclaim as written. Those gaps are now closed and verified against the pinned commit — learning rate 2x10^-4 (`config.py:17`; the harness had used 2x10^-3, a 10x mismatch), AdamW rather than plain Adam (`trainer.py:75`), `batch_size`=28 mini-batching (`config.py:15`; the harness had been full-batch, i.e. exactly one gradient step per epoch), a 5-epoch linear LR warmup during which the scheduler is *not* stepped (`config.py:21`, `trainer.py:88-91,134`), gradient clipping at 0.5, `min_lr`=10^-7, `epochs`=45, `RobustScaler` on the input features and `StandardScaler` on the deep OOF features (`run_pipeline.py:81,85,130`) — alongside the scheduler, early-stopping and checkpoint mechanics already ported (A.1, issue 14). **Not ported, and this is the substantive limit on the word "fidelity":** the audited trainer's EMA of the weights (which it also evaluates and checkpoints under), its composite objective (0.45 BCE + 0.35 focal + 0.20 asymmetric, plus a 0.20-weighted contrastive term on the embedding), BCE `pos_weight` class rebalancing, label smoothing, mixup and cutmix, and the model class itself (their 6-layer, 8-head, 384-hidden multi-scale transformer, against this harness's 3-layer MLP, which is kept so the extension stays comparable to the checkpoint-selection-only harness it is meant to be contrasted with). `use_swa` appears in their `config.py` but is not referenced anywhere in their sources, so there is nothing to port. So: this measures fidelity of the *validation-signal coupling and the optimizer schedule*, not of the loss or the model. It is an estimate of how much continuous scheduler and early-stopping coupling adds over a final-checkpoint argmax within this paper's own harness — not an estimate of MultiHaluDet's own reported number's inflation, which would need their objective and architecture too. The full ported/not-ported list is in `code/49`'s module docstring; A.1 (issue 12) reports the factorial 2x2 ablation separating this extension's two most recent corrections.

### A.9 The pre-registered decision rule

Before running the sweep, we pre-registered a rule classifying each capacity as `GENUINE_LEAK_CONFIRMED`, `CONFOUND_CONFIRMED_NO_REAL_LEAK`, or `MIXED` from the placebo-relative gaps. We initially reported the confound branch as structurally unreachable; that was wrong, and applying the rule to every version actually run: the isotropic synthetic sweep returns `MIXED` at all four capacities; the anisotropic sweep returns `MIXED` at three and `GENUINE_LEAK_CONFIRMED` at one (capacity 128); the ceiling-confounded real-feature test returns `CONFOUND_CONFIRMED_NO_REAL_LEAK` at both tested capacities; the biased-calibration, the leak-contaminated bias-corrected, and the (superseded) label-conditional train-only-centered real-feature tests all return `MIXED` at both capacities; and the current, label-free-calibrated real-feature test returns `GENUINE_LEAK_CONFIRMED` at both. The rule is therefore reachable in every direction — what it is not, is *stable*: the same underlying mechanism produces all three verdicts depending on generative process, calibration, and capacity. We flag this bluntly, including that the verdict moved in the *favourable* direction for our own hypothesis when the calibration was corrected: that is precisely the circumstance in which a single pre-registered rule's output should be trusted least, and it is why this paper's severity claims rest on effect sizes with intervals rather than on the rule's label.

### A.10 Smaller checks, retained for completeness

*Power.* Exact per-seed gap SDs (not back-of-envelope estimates) confirm the real-feature test is adequately powered for the gaps it reports: under the superseded label-conditional calibration the MDEs were 0.00198/0.00154 at capacities 128/384 against observed +0.0021/+0.0022 (adequate but marginal), and under the current label-free calibration the observed +0.0093/+0.0077 sit far above that scale, with BCa intervals excluding zero by a wide margin; earlier, architecturally-mismatched versions were not adequately powered. *Quantization.* A single-point-estimate FP16-vs-AWQ control was re-run with 100 seed-resampled splits through this paper's own severity architecture: gap +0.0068, 95% CI [-0.061,+0.080] (includes zero) – consistent with no confound, not a demonstration ruling one out. *Epoch count.* An anomaly specific to the now-superseded single-fold architecture (CLEAN_MATCHED underperforming PLACEBO by -0.0025) reverses sign under every corrected version (CLEAN_MATCHED outperforms PLACEBO by +0.0157 under the final, train-only-calibrated test) and does not recur; full detail in `results/real_feature_leakage_diagnostics.json`. *Cross-model feature averaging.* An independent review proposed that the small measured severity could be an artifact of the harness's own averaging of five fold-models' test activations rather than a property of the mechanism; §4.3 reports the result in full, and briefly, the alternative is *supported* (`code/55`, `results/oof_averaging_control.json`). *Covariance shape versus dimensionality.* §4.3's isotropic (d=64) and anisotropic (d=414) sweeps differ in two things at once; `code/56` runs the discriminating experiment, holding d=64 and the eigenbasis, the total within-class variance, the mean-difference direction and the calibrated Mahalanobis J all fixed, and varying only the within-class eigenvalue profile. Results and reading are in §4.3.

### A.11 Does severity scale the way extreme-value theory predicts?

**(It does scale with K — but whether it does so in the functional form the theory specifies is a question this harness turns out not to be instrumented to answer.)** The reasoning behind Mechanism 3 is a winner's-curse argument: selecting the best of K noisy validation estimates inflates the reported score by gap~ c*sigma_valsqrt(2ln K), shrinking with validation-set size n_val. `code/47_selection_multiplicity_sweep.py` tests this directly (decoupled `data_seed`/`split_seed`/`fold_seed_base`/ `init_seed_base`, so no single draw can produce all three patterns by coincidence). **Correction 1:** an earlier version of this sweep's synthetic generator had the same calibration bug as Mechanism 5 (issue 11) – corrected using the identical fix and the same non-tautological `sanity_checks.py` guardrail. Sweeping Kin1,3,5,10,15,25,45,75,135,225 at fixed capacity gives a gap that rises essentially monotonically with K (+0.0000 at K=1 to +0.0052 at K=225, with only a brief plateau between K=10 and K=15), and every cell from K=3 upward is individually significant (p<0.0002 throughout).

**Correction 2: the fit as originally reported was not the model the theory states.** `code/47` fits the EVT form with sigma_val replaced by a single *constant* — the mean of the per-cell sigma_val across all ten K values (`code/47`, lines 230-231) — and reports c=0.0417, R^2=0.756. The theory's sigma is the noise SD of the validation estimates *in the cell being predicted*, and the sweep measures exactly that per cell. Substituting the constant silently converts a one-parameter EVT model into "gap is proportional to sqrt(2ln K)," a materially different and much more flattering claim, because the measured per-cell sigma_val varies by 2.66x across the sweep (0.0186 to 0.0494) *and moves in the opposite direction from the gap* (corr(sigma_val,gap)=-0.84, where the theory requires this correlation to be positive). Refit correctly, with each cell's own measured sigma_K (`code/50_evt_scaling_refit.py`, `results/evt_scaling_refit.json`), the EVT form gives c=0.0355, R^2=-0.399 — worse than predicting the mean of the gaps. Two-parameter alternatives fit far better on the same nine cells: a+bln K with a=-0.00012, b=0.00104 gives R^2=0.970, and a* K^b with a=0.00083, b=0.378 gives R^2=0.866.

**Correction 3, which retracts Correction 2's verdict: this is not a falsification, because sigma is the wrong quantity.** A third independent review found that neither the constant-sigma fit nor the per-cell-sigma refit tests extreme-value theory at all. In `code/47`, `mean_val_auc_std` is `np.std(val_aucs)` where `val_aucs` holds one validation AUROC *per epoch of a single training trajectory*. The extreme-value derivation's sigma is the sampling-noise SD of K *exchangeable* estimates scattered around a *common* mean. Successive epochs of one optimizer run are neither: they are a learning curve, and their dispersion is dominated by the systematic climb from initialization to convergence. Both anomalies Correction 2 cited as evidence against the theory follow mechanically from that misspecification, with no need for the theory to be wrong. sigma falls 2.66x as K rises because a longer trajectory spends proportionally more of itself converged and flat, so the SD of the whole curve shrinks — a fact about learning curves, whereas genuine selection noise should be roughly flat in K. And corr(sigma,gap)=-0.84 because the gap rises with K while this sigma falls with K; two quantities driven oppositely by the same variable anticorrelate regardless of whether the theory holds.

**The verdict is therefore downgraded from "falsified" to "untestable in this harness as instrumented."** R^2=-0.399 cannot distinguish "extreme value theory is wrong here" from "sigma is the wrong quantity to test it with," and we no longer assert the former. A real test needs sigma estimated across independent, exchangeable candidate evaluations at a fixed point in training — K independently-seeded models, or repeated resampled validation splits at a fixed epoch — which this harness does not produce. That is a measurement gap rather than a theoretical obstacle, and Limitations (2a) no longer lists it as an obstacle to deriving a formal bound. **What is unaffected:** the empirical law gap=a+bln K at R^2=0.970 never references sigma, and stands exactly as reported, as does every use this paper makes of it. We report all of this as a correction of the previous version's reported fit, which was an artifact of the constant-sigma substitution, not a finding.

**A dynamic-range observation, reported as suggestive rather than decisive.** Across K=3->225 the measured gap grows by a factor of 6.10x (+0.00086->+0.00522), while the sqrt(2ln K) factor grows by only 3.29/1.48=2.22x. If the true sampling-noise sigma were approximately constant across the sweep — which it arguably should be, since n_val is held at 112 throughout — then even with the fitted c free to absorb any overall scale, the functional form would be off by nearly a factor of three in *span*, not merely in fit quality. *We previously presented this as a third independent confirmation of falsification. It is not, for two reasons.* First, its premise is exactly the quantity this harness fails to measure: we cannot verify that the true sigma is constant, because the only sigma available is the trajectory dispersion above. Second, the span comparison is itself confounded by the operating point, which moves from 0.6872 to 0.7574 across those same cells and which §5 shows is a strong severity modifier. So the dynamic-range mismatch is consistent with the extreme-value form being wrong here, and equally consistent with the span being inflated by the operating-point drift. It is listed as an observation, not as evidence.

The scientifically interesting content survives, and is arguably more informative than a clean confirmation would have been: severity does rise with the number of candidates selected among, smoothly and monotonically, and a two-parameter logarithmic law describes it very well (R^2=0.97); whether it does so *through* the sigma_valsqrt(2ln K) channel the winner's-curse heuristic posits, we neither confirm nor exclude.

**Correction 4: Sweep B was mislabelled, and the isolated sweep it should have been reverses the reading.** This appendix and `code/47` both described Sweep B as an "n_val sweep (via `N_SAMPLES`)". It is not one. Varying N_SAMPLESin350,700,2800 moves the training set (280/560/2240), the test set (70/140/560) and the per-fold validation set (56/112/448) simultaneously and proportionally, so nothing it shows can be attributed to n_val. It is relabelled a *sample-size* sweep (`sweep_B_sample_size` in the result JSON, with the confound recorded alongside it), and its numbers are unchanged: +0.0026 (p=0.33), +0.0036 (p=0.0012), +0.0005 (p=0.047). The isolated sweep it should have been costs nothing extra: hold N_SAMPLES=700 fixed — and with it the outer train/test split and the test set the metric is reported on — and vary only the inner CV fold count K_CVin2,3,5,10, which is precisely what sets n_val=n_train/K_CV (`code/47`, Sweep D, n=100 seeds/cell):

### B.2 Case Study 2: the mechanical positivity of Delta_sel

\textbf{A mechanical property of Delta_sel a reader must not over-read.} Delta_sel is positive in expectation even under a pure-noise null: l^star=argmax_l cv_l while gap_l=cv_l-ho_l contains the same +cv_l term, so selecting the argmax of cv necessarily selects a layer whose gap is upward-biased relative to the mean gap. This is not a defect — it is precisely the winner's curse the quantity is constructed to measure. But it means the statistical significance of a measured Delta_sel is *not* evidence of anything beyond argmax-over-a-noisy-statistic. What the measured magnitude adds is a scale for how large that expected effect actually is on this data, and the comparison against the sequential split's apparent +0.188 is what the analysis is for.

**The full 32-layer decomposition.** **Full 32-layer decomposition.** GUARDIAN's raw cache contains the complete (700, 32, 4096) Mistral-7B representations at full dimensionality, not just L11 (an earlier version of this section disclosed this as unavailable; that was incorrect). `code/48` repeats the identical CV-vs-held-out comparison at all 32 layers under GUARDIAN's own sequential `H[:400]`/`H[400:]` split, sanity-checked to reproduce the published L11 numbers exactly. Under this split the mean optimism gap across all 32 layers is +0.192 (SD 0.053), statistically indistinguishable from L11's own +0.188, and 18 of the other 31 layers show a *larger* gap than L11.

Under GUARDIAN's own sequential split, L11 is selected and this component is -0.004 — indistinguishable from zero, so the 18.8-point gap is not disproportionately concentrated at the selected layer once the population-difference confound is present in every layer equally. Reversing which half selects picks a different layer entirely (L17, not L11) and gives +0.074 — a large swing in both which layer is picked and what the component measures, itself direct evidence that the sequential-split number tracks split order rather than a stable property of the selection procedure. Recomputing under randomized stratified 400/300 splits, each rep evaluated at its own selected layer, gives Delta_sel=+0.0255 (SD 0.0186; one-sample t=9.71; Wilcoxon p=7.7x10^-12) — small but, for the first time, correctly measured and distinguishable from zero, about 7x smaller than the sequential split's apparent +0.188. The selected layer spans 14 distinct layers from L5 to L23 across reps, which is itself the argmax instability the component measures. The *general* CV-vs-held-out gap averaged across all 32 layers is +0.192 under the sequential split but only -0.0023 (SD 0.0541, t=-0.30, p=0.76) under the same randomized splits — indistinguishable from zero.

### B.3 Case Study 2: replication count, and what the p-value means

**How many reps, and what the p-value does and does not mean.** An earlier version ran 8 reps and gave no stopping rule, which leaves a reader unable to exclude that 8 was chosen after seeing the result. It is now 50. The binding constraint is not runtime (50 reps takes about 15 minutes on one core) but the size of the shipped replay artifact, which stores every per-layer, per-sample probe score for every rep so `code/51` can re-derive the headline number without the 171 MB raw cache: it grows at roughly 100 KB/rep and reaches 4.0 MB at 50, which is as large as we were willing to put in a submission archive. The RNG stream is unchanged, so the original 8 reps are bit-identical and still give +0.0270 (SD 0.0212); the move to +0.0255 (SD 0.0186) is what tightening the estimate does, not a different measurement. More reps do not, however, fix the statistical problem. All 50 resample splits of the *same* 700 samples, so the SD across reps is split-to-split variability conditional on this one dataset, not sampling variability across independent draws from a population, and the one-sample t-test's i.i.d. assumption is violated. Its p should be read as a within-dataset robustness statistic — "this is not an artifact of which particular split we happened to take" — and *not* as evidence that the effect generalizes beyond GUARDIAN's own 700-sample TruthfulQA sample. The qualitative conclusion (a small, consistently positive selection-specific component, against a general gap indistinguishable from zero) is what we assert. Section~4.4's Mechanism-4 estimate, drawn from 24 independent published result files, is the closest thing in this paper to a genuinely replicated version of the same claim.

### B.4 Case Study 2: probe-configuration robustness

**A robustness check on the probe configuration.** The layer probe here is `LogisticRegression` at d=4096, n=400, `max_iter=1000`, default C=1.0, on unscaled raw hidden states. A review noted that this could plausibly be so heavily regularized toward its prior that both estimates are dominated by regularization rather than fit — which would make "the general gap is ~0" a statement about the probe, not about exchangeability. Two things were checked. *Convergence:* the alternative's premise that `max_iter=1000` might not be reached is false, and measurably so — across the 9,600 probe fits behind the primary result the largest `n_iter_` is 125, with zero `ConvergenceWarning`s. *Regularization:* adding `StandardScaler` and sweeping C over five decades 0.01,0.1,1,10,100 on the same splits leaves both conclusions intact. Delta_sel ranges over +0.0324/+0.0346/+0.0369/+0.0360/+0.0412 (every cell p<3x 10^-5), and the general gap over -0.0044/-0.0017/+0.0001/+0.0027/+0.0088 (SDs ~0.055, every cell indistinguishable from zero). Like-for-like on the same 20 reps, scaling raises Delta_sel modestly, from +0.0306 unscaled to +0.0369 scaled at C=1 — so if anything the unscaled configuration this paper reports is the mildly conservative one, and regularization was not suppressing a general gap that scaling reveals. We report the unscaled result as primary only because it is what GUARDIAN itself ran.

### B.5 Case Study 4: the estimator's algebraic degeneracy

**Two corrections, of which the second is the important one.** An earlier version of this script took the naive baseline from the repo's own reported `best_auroc`, a max over *all three* seeds — including the seed then used as "held-out" — so whenever the leave-one-out argmax coincided with the full-sample argmax (7 of 24 cells), the two sides of the subtraction were arithmetically forced to be equal and the estimate was pinned at exactly 0.0000. A second, independent review then found that rebuilding the estimator as a leave-one-seed-out rotation removed the *stated cause* of that degeneracy while leaving the degeneracy itself exactly intact, in the same 7 of 24 cells: the rotation estimator is *algebraically* pinned to zero whenever all three rotations select the same layer. Writing a_0,a_1,a_2 for the three seeds' AUROC at that common layer L, S=sum_r a_r and bar a=S/3, rotation r selects on the other two seeds, so naive_r=(S-a_r)/2 and honest_r=a_r, giving wc_r=tfrac32(bar a - a_r), whose mean over r is tfrac32(bar a-bar a)=0 *identically, for any data* — the per-rotation values are 1.5x the held-out seed's deviation from the seed mean, pure noise that sums to zero by construction. In those cells the estimator is not measuring a small effect; it is measuring nothing. Two of the seven carried float residues of +/-3.7x10^-17, which `scipy`'s Wilcoxon was ranking as real signed observations rather than dropping as the exact zeros they are. This matters because **all seven degenerate cells fall in the non-saturated subgroup** (7 of its 12), which is the subgroup §5 leans on: its previously-reported +0.0065 was five genuine measurements averaging +0.0157 diluted by seven algebraic zeros, and its Wilcoxon p moves from 0.047 to 0.0625 once the residues are counted as zeros.

**What we did instead of patching the same structure a third time.** `code/45` now (i) *detects and declares* degeneracy per cell, recording the selected layer in each rotation and snapping |*|<10^-12 to exact zero; (ii) reports the headline over the 17 non-degenerate cells only, with the 7 degenerate ones disclosed rather than averaged in; and (iii) characterizes the estimator *including* its degeneracy with a permutation null. A *global* seed relabelling leaves this estimator exactly invariant — it already averages over all three choices of held-out seed — so the shuffle is applied independently *within each layer*, destroying the layerxseed structure that makes an argmax stable while preserving each layer's marginal values. That null is the regime of *maximal* winner's curse (a noise-driven argmax), so it answers the question the degenerate cells cannot: what this estimator would have reported there had the layer ranking been noise. A structurally different second estimator — the bootstrap bias of a max-of-means, which cannot telescope because its two sides use different seed multisets — is reported as a sign-and-magnitude cross-check.

**Excluding the degenerate cells is itself a selection, and we state which way it cuts.** A cell is degenerate exactly when its argmax is stable across all three rotations, and a stable argmax is evidence that the layer ranking there is driven by real structure rather than by noise — the regime in which a winner's curse is *genuinely smaller*. So the 17 surviving cells are plausibly enriched for cells with a larger true effect, and +0.0076 should be read as **the severity among cells where this estimator has resolving power, not as an unbiased average over all 24**. The unbiased-over-24 quantity is not recoverable from three seeds by any estimator of this shape: the honest bracket is that the true all-cell mean lies between the contaminated +0.0054 (which assumes the degenerate cells are exactly zero, and they are not) and +0.0076 (which assumes they resemble the cells the estimator can see, and they probably do not). We report both, label which assumption each makes, and do not split the difference. The bootstrap max-bias cross-check, computable on every cell including the degenerate ones, gives +0.0005 on the degenerate 7 against +0.0027 on the other 17 — consistent with the degenerate cells having a real but smaller effect. **Power, stated rather than left implicit:** the 24 per-cell estimates have SD 0.0079, so the pooled design's minimum detectable effect at alpha=0.05, 80% power is +0.0045. The non-degenerate non-saturated subgroup is now n=5 and is plainly underpowered on its own; we report it descriptively and do not lean on its p=0.0625.

### B.6 Case Study 4: ceiling composition of the 24 cells

**Ceiling composition of the 24 cells.** These cells are not comparable to each other, and reporting only their pooled mean hides that. In 12 of 24 the *selected* layer already sits at AUROC >=0.975 (all six `halueval_qa` cells and all six `synthetic` cells), leaving almost no headroom for a selection effect to be visible; the remaining 12 (`fever`, `truthfulqa`) are genuinely non-saturated. Reported separately, with the degeneracy split shown rather than buried: ceiling-saturated +0.0042 (CI [+0.0029,+0.0057], 12 cells, *none* degenerate); non-saturated +0.0065 (CI [+0.0022,+0.0154], p=0.0625) across all 12 of its cells, but +0.0157 (CI [+0.0074,+0.0276], p=0.0625) across the 5 the estimator can actually see. **The honest reading of the saturated-versus-non-saturated contrast is therefore the second pair:** +0.0042 against +0.0157, a 3.7x difference, not the +0.0042 against +0.0065 the contaminated numbers suggested. By dataset: `fever` +0.0124, `halueval_qa` +0.0052, `synthetic` +0.0033, `truthfulqa` +0.0007 (the last is 0.0000 in four of its six cells for the degeneracy reason above, and should not be read as a dataset effect). Under a stricter saturation reading (every layer, not just the selected one, above 0.975 – true for the six `halueval_qa` cells) the split is +0.0052 (6 cells) versus +0.0054 (18 cells). The mechanism's severity is small on every slicing, and larger where there is room for it to show, consistent with the operating-point relationship in §5 — though §5 also reports the limits of how far that relationship transports.

### B.7 Mechanism 5: sample-size dependence of the F1 and accuracy gaps

**These gaps are a function of sample size, and the sample sizes were fixed and unstated.** All five cells above run at N=700, hence n_test=140 (LEAKY's selection set) and n_val=112 (HONEST's). That matters because a threshold chosen by argmax over an 81-point grid is a winner's curse over a *noisy* criterion, and the noise scales with 1/sqrt(n) — so the magnitude of this gap is not a property of the mechanism alone. **It also means the phrase "MultiHaluDet's own reported regime" above matches their operating point and *not* their sample size**, which their released configuration does not pin: `config.py` fixes only `test_size`=0.20, while `src/data/loader.py` defaults `max_samples`=10000, so their n_test could be more than an order of magnitude larger than 140. Holding the operating point at 0.985 and scaling N (`code/46`, `SWEEP=N`, 200 seeds/cell) gives an F1 gap of +0.0212 at n_test=140, +0.0111 at 350, +0.0067 at 700 and +0.0034 at 2000 — a **6.2x decline**, close to the 3.8x a pure 1/sqrt(n) account would give, so most but not all of it is selection-noise scaling. Accuracy behaves the same way (+0.0215 to +0.0036). Every cell remains overwhelmingly significant (p<10^-31 throughout, CIs excluding zero), so the mechanism does not vanish — but \textbf{the +0.021 to +0.034 range this paper quotes for Mechanism 5 is an estimate at n_test=140, and a pipeline reporting at a test-set size ten times larger should expect roughly a third of it.} We report the n_test=140 figures as the headline only because they are the ones measured at all five operating points; they are not a size-matched estimate of MultiHaluDet's own inflation, and we do not present them as one.

## Appendix C. Extended Detail for the Severity Surface

### C.1 The degenerate K=3 column, and the degeneracy gate that replaced it

**A degenerate corner of the first version of this grid, and why K=3 is no longer in it.** The grid originally ran Kin3,15,45,135, and on it the interaction term of §5.5 was significant (F(1,16)=5.92, p=0.027), which an earlier version of this paper promoted to the abstract as "the two axes interact multiplicatively rather than additively." A later independent review showed that result came *entirely* from the K=3 column, and that the K=3 column is degenerate. At three epochs there are too few candidates for the two selection rules to diverge: LEAKY argmaxes validation AUROC over three epochs on the reused fold, CLEAN_MATCHED argmaxes over the same three on a disjoint carve-out, both almost always land on the last epoch, and CLEAN_MATCHED's retrain then reproduces LEAKY's optimizer trajectory step for step — leaving the two models *bitwise identical* in 81–100% of folds, so the "gap" there is 0.00000 by construction rather than by measurement. (Two of the old grid's twenty cells, K=3 at AUROC_0=0.95 and 0.985, had gap exactly 0.00000: the signature.) Refitting the old grid without the K=3 column moves the interaction test from p=0.027 to p=0.378, while dropping any *other* K column leaves it significant (p=0.043, 0.041, 0.044). The interaction was a property of the degenerate corner, not of the surface. So K=3 is replaced by K=405, keeping four points on a wider range than before; and every cell now carries a **degeneracy record** — the fraction of folds in which the two arms' `state_dict()` tensors are bitwise identical, the largest parameter difference anywhere in the cell, and how often each arm's argmax lands on the last epoch — with the fit *gated* on it and refusing to run if any surviving cell exceeds 50%. The old K=3 column is re-run under the same instrumentation and ships as evidence for its own removal, not as a fitted cell.

**The gate is not a clean bill of health for the whole corrected grid.** Degeneracy is a matter of degree and decays with K rather than switching off: across the corrected grid the bitwise-identical fraction is 1–5% for Kin45,135,405 but 24–42% for K=15, worst at (K=15,AUROC_0=0.90). K=15 therefore passes the gate but is the column carrying the least contrast. Every conclusion in §5.5 is accordingly refit five ways — on all 20 cells and with each K column deleted in turn — and we report the range rather than the single number (`drop_one_K_column_sensitivity` in the result JSON); the same deletion test is what exposed the old grid's headline. *One further disclosure on this grid's consistency.* The (K=45,AUROC_0=0.80) configuration has two legitimate values in this paper, because it is run at two sample sizes: Sweep A uses n=200 seeds and gives +0.004155, while Sweeps B/C/D and this grid all use n=100 and give +0.003649. They differ only by Monte Carlo error over seeds 100–199 (seeds 0–99 are shared, so the two are not independent). We state the n=200 value rather than let a reader find two numbers for one cell and infer a drift.

### C.2 The joint fit's stability under column deletion

**What this says.** A single joint model does describe this surface well, and it is the multiplicative one: severity factors, to a good approximation, into a candidate-count term and an operating-point term multiplied together, gap~ A* K^be^cz_0. That form is *separable* — it contains no interaction term — so its excellence relative to the additive model is a statement about the *scale* on which the two axes combine, not evidence that they interact, and **we therefore withdraw the previous version's claim that "the two axes interact multiplicatively rather than additively."** The conclusion survives the test that killed its predecessor: deleting each K column in turn, the interaction stays non-significant every time (p=0.18, 0.22, 0.38, 0.64) and the multiplicative fit stays strong (R^2=0.967–0.994), with a stable operating-point coefficient (c=-2.80 to -3.17) and an unstable candidate-count exponent (b=0.09 to 0.21, lowest when the K=15 column is removed) — consistent with K being the weaker axis and K=15 the column with least contrast. We quote b as a range rather than a point wherever it matters.

**This forces a qualification of one of this paper's own headline numbers.** The gap=a+bln K law with R^2=0.970 reported above is measured along a single row of this grid, at AUROC_0=0.80. Across the grid as a whole, ln K *alone* explains essentially none of the variance (R^2=0.038, and at LOO -0.192 it is worse than predicting the mean). The K-law is therefore real *within* an operating point and not a description of severity in general — the table shows a 4–7x span across K where there is headroom and essentially none at AUROC_0=0.985, every cell there sitting between +0.00012 and +0.00019 regardless of whether 15 or 405 candidates were tried. This qualification is *stronger* on the corrected grid than on the degenerate one it replaces (R^2=0.038 against 0.114), so unlike the interaction claim it does not depend on the removed cells. Candidate count is a second-order modifier of a first-order operating-point effect, not a co-equal axis.

### C.3 The joint fit's out-of-sample check

**The out-of-sample check is also weaker than the previous version of this paper presented it as.** The intended check predicts the five Sweep A cells at Kin5,10,25,75,225, which are not in the grid. Two things undercut it, both found by instrumenting rather than by argument. First, *two of those five held-out cells are themselves degenerate*: at K=5 the LEAKY and CLEAN_MATCHED arms are bitwise identical in 90% of folds and at K=10 in 65%, for exactly the reason K=3 was dropped, so they are not valid targets. Second, Sweep A runs 200 seeds while this grid runs 100, and at these effect sizes that matters more than the models do — the same (K=225,AUROC_0=0.80) configuration gives +0.00522 at n=200 and +0.00393 at n=100, a 33% difference. For the record the numbers are R^2=0.279 (additive), 0.795 (interaction) and 0.634 (multiplicative) over all five, and 0.365, 0.567 and -0.249 over the three non-degenerate ones. We read nothing into the ordering: the interaction model scoring highest out-of-sample while being non-significant in-sample is exactly the pattern this seed-count mismatch would produce by chance, and treating it as support for the interaction would repeat, in a subtler form, the error §5.5 already corrects. This harness does not currently support an out-of-sample test of the joint surface, and building one would mean re-running Sweep A at matched seed counts on non-degenerate K values.

## Appendix D. Extended Detail for the Checklist and the Automated Scanner

### D.1 Benchmarking against existing leakage taxonomies and checklists

**Kapoor & Narayanan's cross-field leakage taxonomy.** The closest prior art to this paper's central move is Kapoor & Narayanan (2023), who survey leakage across 17 scientific fields and 294 papers and propose an 8-type taxonomy: L1.1 (no test set), L1.2 (pre-processing computed on train+test combined), L1.3 (feature selection on train+test), L1.4 (duplicates across the split), L2 (illegitimate/proxy features), L3.1 (temporal leakage), L3.2 (non-independence between train and test samples), and L3.3 (test distribution not matching the distribution of scientific interest). Mapping this paper's five mechanisms onto it: Mechanisms 4 and 5 (test-set-driven best-layer and threshold selection) are both direct cases of **L1.3** — the test set is reused for a modeling decision and then for the reported metric, leaving no genuinely held-out data behind that decision. (An earlier draft of this paper mapped both to L1.1, "no test set" – that was wrong: these pipelines do construct a test split and do train on a disjoint set; what they do illegitimately is *select* using the test split, which is precisely L1.3's scope, not L1.1's.) Mechanism 1 (full-dataset fit-then-score feature leakage) is an instance of L1.2, generalized from a preprocessing statistic to an entire fitted feature-extraction model, and Mechanism 3 (per-fold checkpoint selection) is a narrower-bandwidth variant of the same pattern, restricted to which training iterate is kept. Mechanism 2 (CV-based layer/hyperparameter selection optimism, i.e. Varma & Simon's problem) does not map cleanly onto any single one of the eight leaf types: it is closest in spirit to L1.2/L1.3 but is really a distinct, model-selection-specific pattern their taxonomy does not name explicitly. That asymmetry — three of this paper's five mechanisms are instances of an existing general taxonomy, while Case Study 2's pattern is not one of its eight named types — is consistent with this paper's claim that hidden-state probing's two-stage structure surfaces at least one leakage pattern general cross-field taxonomies do not yet enumerate by name.

**Benchmarking against REFORMS and the ML Reproducibility Checklist.** REFORMS (Kapoor et al. 2024, *Science Advances*) is a 32-item, 8-module consensus checklist for ML-based science: study goals (3 items), computational reproducibility (5), data quality (7), data preprocessing (3), modeling (6), data leakage (3), metrics and uncertainty quantification (3), and generalizability and limitations (2). Its dedicated data-leakage module covers exactly three questions – train/test separation, dependencies between train and test instances, and feature legitimacy – all three subsumed by this paper's five-mechanism taxonomy; and Mechanism 2 again falls outside its scope, for the same reason it falls outside Kapoor & Narayanan's leaf types, since that module treats train/test separation and feature legitimacy as the leakage surface rather than iterative model/architecture selection against a validation signal. REFORMS' other seven modules cover reporting practices this paper does not audit (study-goal framing, uncertainty quantification beyond the specific severity estimates here, external-validity discussion), so neither instrument is a superset of the other. The NeurIPS Machine Learning Reproducibility Checklist (Pineau et al. 2021, *JMLR*) is reporting-practice-oriented rather than leakage-specific: its four sections ask whether code, hyperparameters, dataset statistics, compute budgets, and multi-seed variance are disclosed, none of which individually detects a leakage bug. This paper's own reproducibility practice (full per-seed result arrays, vendored external repositories, BCa bootstrap/permutation tests on every severity gap) satisfies the bulk of the Pineau checklist's disclosure items as a byproduct of the statistical rigor the severity claims already require — but that checklist would not, on its own, have caught any of the fourteen issues this paper's own Appendix A documents finding and fixing in its own instrument. Reporting-practice checklists and leakage-detection checklists are complementary, not interchangeable, and a paper can satisfy the former while still committing the latter.

### D.2 The blinded second-rater protocol

**How the hits were classified, and the reliability limits of that classification.** A further independent review pointed out that the true-positive/false-positive labels were, as originally produced, a single rater's manual reading; that the rater was this paper's author; and that the rater already knew where both real bugs were. That is three distinct threats at once — no inter-rater reliability, no blinding, and an interested rater — and none was disclosed. We state them, and we did something about the first two: an independent second reading of the same 7 sites was obtained under a protocol that withheld this paper's draft, its checklist, and the location of the two known bugs, giving the second rater only the flagged file/line/snippet and the vendored third-party source. **The second rater was a language model, not a second human**, which bounds what the agreement is worth: it checks that the classification survives a reader with no stake in the result and no knowledge of the answer, not that two domain experts converge. **Agreement was 7 of 7 — the second rater classified every one of the seven as a false positive, with the same provenance argument** (`haloscope` selects on `gt_label_val` from `wild_q_indices2`, provably disjoint from the test indices; `embed_viz.py` fits a projection whose output reaches only a matplotlib call). Percent agreement is therefore 100%; *Cohen's kappa is undefined here*, because both raters used a single category, and we report the raw agreement rather than a chance-corrected coefficient that this degenerate marginal makes uncomputable. Two-rater agreement on seven non-independent sites from two repositories is a weak reliability check, not a strong one.

Two further outcomes of that blind read cut in different directions. *Supporting the paper:* asked separately, and without being told where any bug was, to read the two case-study repositories for leakage, the second rater independently located both known true positives — `run_pipeline.py:139-141` (Mechanism 5) and `saplma.py:35-41` together with its duplicate at `src/analysis/metrics.py:85-91` (Mechanism 4) — direct evidence that the two false negatives (D.3) are failures of the *scanner* rather than artifacts of the authors' privileged knowledge of where to look. *Cutting against it:* the same rater declined to count MultiHaluDet's `trainer.py` checkpoint selection (Mechanism 3) as qualifying, on the ground that the leaked fold's features go on to train the meta-learner against `y_train` only, so the reported test metric is not inflated *directly*; the rater called it "a mild stacking impurity" instead. We disagree — §4.3 measures a small but real and, at two of four capacities, significant inflation from exactly this path — but the disagreement is genuine, it is about whether a site clears a binary threshold rather than about what the code does, and it is precisely the kind of judgement a single non-blinded rater cannot surface. It is also consistent with our own finding that this mechanism's magnitude is at the low end of the band.

The blind read also surfaced two sites in `HallucinationPatternDetection` that this paper had *not* previously reported and that we verified directly against the pinned commit. (i) `scripts/06_analyze_attention.py:33-52` computes a per-layer AUROC over the *entire labelled set with no split at all*, takes `best = max(per_layer, key=...auroc)`, and writes `best_auroc` into the metrics JSON that `scripts/07_aggregate_results.py:74-86` folds into the headline table — Mechanism 4 in a purer form than the `saplma.py` site, since there is no held-out split behind it at all. (ii) `src/analysis/metrics.py:57-65` (`score_to_metrics`) picks a Youden threshold by `np.argmax(tpr-fpr)` on the same labels it then scores `accuracy_at_best_thr` and `f1_at_best_thr` against, and `07_aggregate_results.py:85-86` reports both — Mechanism 5, in the *second* audited repository. **So Mechanism 5 is not specific to MultiHaluDet**: the same test-label-tuned operating-point selection occurs independently in both externally published pipelines this paper audits, which we did not know when §4.5 was written. Neither new site changes any severity number here — both are additional instances of mechanisms already quantified — and neither is included in §6.1's 7-hit count, which is the scanner's output, not a human audit's.

### D.3 Why the scanner fails: two specific causes

**The scanner does not work, for two specific reasons.** It produced 7 raw hits, concentrated in 2 repos, and manual reading showed all 7 are false positives: 3 flag `HallucinationPatternDetection`'s `embed_viz.py` for "fit-then-score" – these are t-SNE/UMAP/PCA calls used only for plotting; 4 flag `haloscope`'s layer/threshold selection – reading the code shows selection correctly uses a separate validation split, the opposite of leakage, and a positive data point for that NeurIPS 2024 pipeline. More tellingly, it missed both known true positives, and **both misses have identifiable causes in this scanner's own construction.** (i) Case Study 3's selection site (`trainer.py`, lines 137-144) binds `best_auc`/`best_model`; the scanner's `CHECKPOINT_KEYWORDS` list (`code/04`, line 55) contains only `best_epoch`, `best_checkpoint` and `checkpoint`. This is a plain keyword-list gap – and a telling one, since we had already read those exact two token names while manually diagnosing the bug and still did not add them. (ii) Case Study 4's selection site (`saplma.py`, lines 32-41) *is* matched by `ARGMAX_KEYWORDS`, which already contains `best_layer`; what fails is the scanner's design choice to require an argmax token and a `test`-named variable *on the same line*, and, before that, in the same file. That file contains no `test`-named identifier anywhere – the test split lives one call away, in `probes.py::train_layerwise_probes` – so a same-file, same-line conjunction cannot fire on it however the keyword lists are extended. Zero true positives among the 5 newly-scanned repos, two false negatives on the two known positives, seven-for-seven false positives on its raw hits: this particular scanner does not work. We deliberately do not read that as evidence that the leakage class is inherently resistant to static detection. Cause (i) would be fixed by a longer keyword list, and cause (ii) points at a specific, tractable design requirement – the argmax and the test-derived metric must be connected across statements and across function boundaries, i.e. by a dataflow analysis rather than line matching. What this exercise establishes is narrower and more useful: *line-level regex matching* is the wrong tool here, because every one of these mechanisms is a relationship between a selection statement and the provenance of the quantity it selects on, and that relationship is routinely split across lines, functions, and files.

### D.4 One data point on a non-regex approach

**One data point on what a non-regex approach can do, and why we do not generalize from it either.** The three obvious alternatives to line-level regex are an AST pass, an interprocedural dataflow analysis, and a language-model read of the source. We built none of the first two. The blinded second-rater protocol above is, incidentally, an instance of the third — a language model reading the vendored source with no access to this paper — and it located both true positives in the two known-buggy repositories, plus the two additional sites reported in D.2, where the regex scanner found none. That is one run, on two repositories already known to contain bugs, with no false-positive rate measured on the five presumed-clean repos and no protocol for what it should be asked or how its output should be adjudicated. It is enough to say the failure above is *this scanner's*, not the failure of automated detection as a category — which is all §6 claims — and nowhere near enough to say a language-model scanner works. Measuring that properly, on a pre-registered corpus with both classes present, is the same follow-on work.
